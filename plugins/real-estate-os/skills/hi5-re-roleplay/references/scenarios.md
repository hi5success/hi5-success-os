# Scenarios, Scoring, and Debrief

Used by `SKILL.md`. Practice only. Stay in character, one message per turn, and wait for the member's reply.

## The scenarios
Offer the list, and let the member choose or say "pick for me". Each scenario uses the member's market, niche, proof points, and Objection Bank, and a situation and personality, never a protected trait.

**Sellers and listings**
1. **The listing appointment.** A seller interviewing several agents. Practice rapport, discovery, the pricing conversation, and the ask.
2. **The fee conversation.** A seller who says another agent will do it for less, or asks the member to cut their fee. Compensation is set by agreement and is negotiable. The member never quotes a standard rate.
3. **The seller who wants to list high.** An owner who wants a price above the comps. Practice the honest pricing conversation with the member's own comps and no guarantees.
4. **A price reduction talk.** A seller whose listing has been quiet. Practice an honest, blame-free conversation (see /hi5-re-seller-updates).

**Buyers and offers**
5. **The buyer consultation.** A buyer who is unsure how the process works. Practice discovery on property needs only.
6. **The buyer agreement conversation.** A buyer who asks "why do I have to sign this?" and "can you do it for less?". Practice the plain explanation of the member's own agreement and the compensation answer (set by agreement, negotiable, never a standard rate).
7. **The buyer who wants to wait.** A buyer waiting on rates or the market. The member never predicts rates or prices.
8. **A multiple-offer talk.** Buyers who want to waive protections or write a personal letter. Practice laying out risks, leaving the decision to the buyer, and the Fair Housing caution about personal letters.

**Prospecting**
9. **A cold call to an expired or FSBO owner.** A practice call only, with no real call made. The persona is guarded or busy. Begin the scenario with the reminder that a real call needs a scrub against the National Do-Not-Call Registry, the state list, and the member's own list, and the 8am to 9pm window. Practice the opener, permission to continue, and a graceful exit when told no, including honoring "do not call me again".

**Other client types**
10. **An investor.** A buyer who wants the member to promise a return. The member never states returns.
11. **A tenant or a landlord.** Practice a tenant representation conversation or a landlord leasing conversation. Screening criteria are written and applied the same way to everyone.
12. **A steering request.** A client who asks about who lives in an area or wants to avoid a kind of neighbor. Practice the compliant redirect: describe the property, point to the school district and the local police department's public data for schools and safety (never demographic or census data about who lives there, and never a rating site), say that Fair Housing rules keep the member from describing who lives in an area, and keep the relationship. The persona stays polite and never gives a protected-trait statement for the member to repeat.

If the member asks for something else (for example a new agent recruiting conversation or a conversation with an agent on the other side), build the scenario from what they describe, keeping to the same rules.

## Setup (modes 1 and 3)
Ask only what is missing after the scan: the scenario, the difficulty, and an optional focus (pricing, discovery, the close, a particular objection). Difficulty:
- **1:** friendly, asks easy questions, accepts a reasonable answer.
- **2:** curious, one real objection.
- **3:** skeptical, raises 3 objections and wants proof.
- **4:** pushes back, compares to other agents, challenges every claim.
- **5:** pushes back on almost everything, changes the subject, and does not accept a weak answer.
Start in character with the first line. Do not narrate and do not explain the scenario inside the character.

## Mode 2: prep for a real conversation
1. Read the upcoming appointment from the calendar or CRM (read only), or ask the member to describe it. Use only property and situation facts: what they are trying to do, the timeline, the price range, and anything the member shares about their concerns. Leave out names, protected traits, and sensitive details, and say so.
2. Show the plan: the likely 3 to 5 objections for this conversation (from the member's Objection Bank and the situation), the member's strongest proof point, one question to ask early, and the line the member is most likely to get wrong.
3. Run a custom practice built on that situation (a persona from the situation and personality only), at the difficulty the member picks, then the debrief.
4. If the conversation is a listing appointment or a buyer consultation, point to /hi5-re-listing-appt or /hi5-re-buyer for the full kit.

## Mode 3: a five-minute drill
Pick one weak spot from the member's last debrief (or ask which one). Run 3 short rounds on that one thing, for example the compensation answer or the close. After each reply give a one-line score, the strongest phrase, and a stronger version in the member's voice under 4 sentences. End with the one habit to keep.

## The debrief (after every practice)
Break character and give:
1. **Scores out of 10** for rapport, discovery, objection handling, clarity, compliance, and the ask, each with a one-line reason. If the member made a promise, quoted a standard fee, steered, or stated an unprovided claim, name it, quote it, and score compliance accordingly.
2. **The moment you lost them,** quoting the member's line, and what would have worked better.
3. **The strongest line,** quoted.
4. **A rewrite of the weakest answer** in the member's voice, under 5 sentences, with no guarantee, no standard rate, and no invented number. Use the member's Objection Bank answer and public proof point when they exist, or [ADD PROOF].
5. **Habits:** 3 to keep and 2 to fix.
6. **One drill** to run before the real conversation.
Be specific and kind, and never say a score is a prediction of how a real person will respond.

## Never
Never invent a client story or a number, never quote a standard commission, never promise a price or a result, never play a persona built on a protected trait, never model abusive language, and never contact anyone or change a record.
