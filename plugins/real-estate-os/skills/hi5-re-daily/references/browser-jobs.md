# Jobs 8 to 11: Optional Browser-Assisted Market Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. These four jobs use data from the member's own MLS. Read `../../hi5-re-crm/references/guardrails.md` section 4 first. Everything else in `jobs.md` still applies: scan first, ask only about what is unknown, read only, and no Fair Housing steering.

Say exactly one line at the start of each job: "Be sure to check your MLS rules." Add nothing else about MLS rules, and do not explain why.

## How the data gets in
Offer two ways, and let the member choose. They can switch at any time.
- **A) Export or paste (always available).** The member exports a report or a CSV from their MLS the normal way, or copies the results, and pastes or uploads it here. Work only from that.
- **B) Browser-assisted search (optional).** The member opens their own MLS in the browser and logs in themselves, with Claude for Chrome in "Ask before acting" mode. Before any search, show the exact search criteria you plan to use and wait for the member's OK. Read only: never edit, save, share, or email anything in the MLS. Use only what is visible on the pages the member approved. Do not crawl, bulk-collect, or click through other listings. The member watches, and if the MLS blocks the step or shows a prompt, stop and switch to A.
This applies to the member's own MLS login only. Never use a browser tool on Zillow, Redfin, or any other listing site, and never for anything else. Do not copy photos or listing descriptions into the member's marketing. If a number is based on very few sales, say so. If a field is not shown, write "not shown" and never fill a gap with a guess.

## 8. Neighborhood market pulse
Inputs to look for: the area (neighborhood or subdivision, and city), the window (30, 60, or 90 days), the property type and price band, and the audience (a seller the member is meeting, or a general client update). Scan the profile and the Neighborhood page first.
From the closed sales in the window, work out:
1. The number of sales and the median sale price.
2. The average and median days to contract.
3. The sale to list ratio (against the original list price and the final list price).
4. Seller concessions, the range and how often, only if the MLS reports it.
5. Price reductions: the share of sales that had one and the average size.
6. What the fast, clean sales had in common (pricing, condition, prep, photos, timing), and what the slow ones had in common.
Then write a one page briefing, "What's really happening in [area] right now": 3 headline takeaways in plain language, a "What this means if you're selling" section with 3 practical points, one "mistake sellers are making right now" backed by the data, and a note on sample size if there are fewer than 10 sales. Describe homes and market conditions, never who buyers are. End with: "Source: [MLS name], [date range]. Information deemed reliable but not guaranteed."

## 9. Is the competition priced right?
Inputs to look for: the area, the property type and size band, the closed window (90 days by default), and the purpose (pricing the member's listing, advising a buyer, or market research).
1. Show the search for active and closed listings and wait for the member's OK (method B), or work from the export.
2. Closed sales: the median price, the median price per square foot, the sale to list ratio, and the median days to contract.
3. Actives: a table of Address, List price, Price per square foot, Days on market, Price changes, and versus closed price per square foot (above, in line, or below).
4. Flag every active that looks likely to sit, with a one line reason (price per square foot against sold, days on market already past the median, reductions, or condition noted in the remarks).
5. A 3 bullet bottom line: where the market is clearing, the price band that is moving, and the band that is stalling.
Compare like homes only and say when a comp is a stretch. Do not guess at condition you cannot see. With fewer than 5 closed comps, say the read is weak.

## 10. Buyer alert matching
Inputs to look for: the buyer's search criteria (price, beds, baths, must-haves, area) from the CRM record or the member, the buyer's name for a one-to-one version, and the window (the last 7 days by default). The criteria must be the buyer's own stated property criteria. Never add or remove listings by who lives in an area, and never steer. If a buyer asks about schools or safety, give only official sources and facts the member provides.
1. Show the search and wait for the member's OK (method B), or work from the export.
2. List each new listing: Address, Price, Beds and baths, Square feet, Days on market, and one line on what stands out (a feature, a price per square foot edge, or a lot detail).
3. Rank them by fit to the criteria and name the top pick and why.
4. Write the buyer email: a subject line that names the top pick's standout feature, the strongest listing in 2 sentences, short bullets for the rest (5 at most), one call to action (reply with which ones to see this week), under 150 words. Describe the homes, not who they are right for. Add a placeholder for the member's disclosure and any listing courtesy line the MLS requires.
5. Show the draft in the chat. Save it as a Gmail draft only with the member's OK, and never send. To send, use /hi5-re-crm job 11.

## 11. Competing listing positioning
Inputs to look for: the competing listing (only the one the member has open in their MLS, or the details they exported or pasted) and the member's seller's home (address, price or range, beds and baths, square feet, top features, and condition notes). Check the profile and earlier work for this seller first.
1. **Competitor snapshot:** the list price, original price and any reductions, days on market, the number of photos with notes on their quality, and the angle the description leads with (price, features, lifestyle, or urgency).
2. **Strengths:** what they do well that buyers will notice.
3. **Vulnerabilities:** where they are exposed (price per square foot, stale days on market, weak photos, or a missing feature).
4. **The member's positioning:** the 3 things the seller's home can claim that this one cannot, a suggested lead angle, and a pricing note on how the seller's price would read next to theirs, for the member's CMA conversation and not as a final price.
5. **A listing presentation script** of 4 to 6 sentences to say to the seller, without trashing the other listing or its agent.
Only read what is visible on the one page the member has open, and do not click through other listings. Do not copy their photos or description text into the member's marketing. If a field is not shown, write "not shown".
