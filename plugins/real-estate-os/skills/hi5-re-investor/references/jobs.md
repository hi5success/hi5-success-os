# The Six Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (the CRM record and notes, the profile, earlier Marketing Hub rows for this investor, and the investor's own figures and documents), ask only about what could not be found, and point to what you found. The investor owns every number and every assumption. Never state a return, yield, market rent, or "good deal". Drafts only. If something important is missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

---

## Start

### 1. The buy box intake
Inputs to look for: the investor's goal (hold, rent, renovate and sell, or a mix), timeline, price range, property types, unit counts, condition they will take on, areas they chose (a city, a radius around an address, or named areas), financing (cash or loan, and whether they have talked to a lender who works with investors), who else decides, and how they will manage the property. Read the CRM first and ask only about what is missing.
Build a one-page Buy Box: the goal and timeline in the investor's words, the criteria as measurable filters, deal-breakers, what is unknown with the question to ask, and what the member does next (the search plan with /hi5-re-buyer job 4, and the numbers worksheet). Offer to log the buy box with /hi5-re-crm after the member's OK.

### 2. The deal numbers worksheet
Build a worksheet the investor fills in for one property: purchase price and costs the investor expects, financing terms (down payment, rate, term, as the lender or investor states them), rent by unit and its source (lease, rent roll, or a rental comp report the member supplies), vacancy assumption, and each expense line (taxes, insurance, maintenance, management, utilities, reserves) with its source. Then compute only the metrics the investor chose (for example net operating income and cash flow before debt service, or a cap rate if the investor uses one), with every step shown and every input labeled "your number" and its source. Missing inputs stay blank, and the output says which results cannot be calculated yet. End with: "These are your assumptions, not a forecast. Review them with your CPA and lender."

---

## Compare and check

### 3. A property comparison
Inputs to look for: two to five properties and the investor's numbers for each (from job 2). Build a side by side table on the buy box criteria (price, units, condition, areas as the investor chose, and any metric the investor chose, each with its source), and a short note on how each matches the buy box. Rank only if the investor tells you the order of their priorities, and then say it ranks the investor's own criteria. Never recommend a property, and never describe an area by its people.

### 4. The due diligence checklist
Build a checklist for the property type, each item with an owner (the investor, the member, an inspector, the lender, the title company, an attorney, or a CPA) and a due date from the contract or "confirm with contract": the contract and any addenda, leases, the rent roll, tenant estoppels and notices as the documents require, security deposits held, income and expense statements, tax and insurance quotes, inspections (property, pests, and systems), survey and title, permits and code questions for the local office, utilities and who pays, the property manager's terms, and the attorney, CPA, and lender steps. Point to /hi5-re-crm documents reading for any lease, and /hi5-re-transaction for the file. Never state what a lease or a law provides, and never give a tenant-rights or notice answer.

---

## Plan and keep in touch

### 5. The portfolio sale and exchange timeline
Inputs to look for: the property being sold, the investor's goal, and any dates the investor, their CPA, or their qualified intermediary gave. Build a timeline that fits those dates: the listing prep, the marketing period the member plans, the contract and closing steps, and the points where the investor needs their CPA, intermediary, and attorney (marked "confirm with your CPA and qualified intermediary"). If the investor mentions a 1031 exchange, say it has strict deadlines that their advisors set, and ask only for their dates. Never state, compute, or advise on a deadline or a tax result. For the listing itself, point to /hi5-re-listing-appt (the investor seller module).

### 6. Investor updates and nurture
Write a short update (under 150 words) with what the member did, what is new against the Buy Box (only listings the member supplies), the investor's next decision, and a closing line. Numbers appear only as the investor gave them. For an investor who is not buying yet, build a 6-month plan with a monthly email (a useful resource the member has, a "what would change your timeline" question, and a re-qualify check), email by default, and texts only where the gate passes. Never state market figures or predictions the member did not give, and never promise deal flow.
