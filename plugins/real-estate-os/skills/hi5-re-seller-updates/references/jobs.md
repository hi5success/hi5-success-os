# The Twelve Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (the profile, the CRM Map, the CRM record for this listing if the CRM is connected and the member allows a read, earlier Marketing Hub rows for this address, and what the member pasted), then ask only for what could not be found, and point to what you found. Apply the feedback filter, the Fair Housing scan, and the safeguards. Drafts only. If something important is still missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

Listing facts to look for before asking: the address, the seller's name, the list date and price history, days on market and the week number, showings, second showings, online views or saves if the member has them, open house traffic, offers, showing feedback, competing listings, recent sales and pendings in the area, what the member did this week, and the member's recommendation.

---

## Start

### 1. Pre-listing prep plan
Inputs to look for: the seller names, the property, the photo date, the target go-live date, the prep items noted at the walkthrough, what the member is handling, and the prep budget.
Write: a subject line plus 2 alternates, a short opener that celebrates the decision and states the two dates, a checklist in three groups (**Must do before photos**, **Should do if time allows**, **Skip it**, things that will not change how buyers see the home), each item a single line with a due date, "What I'm handling" in 3 to 5 bullets, photo-day reminders (pets, cars, personal items, lights on, blinds open), and a closing line with how to reach the member and when they check in next. Under 300 words and scannable on a phone.
Before finalizing, make sure every due date falls before the photo date, and that nothing needing a permit or a licensed trade is listed without saying so.

---

## Weekly rhythm

### 2. This week's seller update
Choose the mode from the numbers:
- **Active week:** lead with the activity and what it means.
- **Slow or zero-showing week:** say so plainly in the first two sentences, never spin it, explain what the member is doing and what the market shows, and, if the recommendation calls for it, plant the seed for a price or condition conversation without making the full ask in writing.
Structure: a subject line, one human opening line (not "Hope you're well"), the numbers in one short paragraph, feedback themes (after the feedback filter) that are honest and constructive with what buyers are comparing the home to, one market insight for context, and the recommendation with a specific next step and date (a call, a decision, or nothing needed). Under 200 words, short paragraphs, like a personal note. For a rental, report showings, applications, and the lease-up timeline.
Before finalizing, check every number against what the member gave and that nothing promises results. Offer to log the update in the CRM with /hi5-re-crm (job 4), and send it with /hi5-re-crm (job 11), after the member's OK.

### 3. A showing feedback request
For a buyer's agent who showed the listing. Write the main message under 50 words that thanks them and asks three things answerable in a few words: (a) interest level 1 to 5, (b) the one thing that held the buyer back, if anything, and (c) whether the price seemed right, high, or low for what they saw. Number the questions so they can reply "4, kitchen, high". Then a follow-up for 48 hours later under 25 words, and a thank-you reply under 20 words with a note that any offer interest will be passed along right away.
Friendly, easy to answer from a phone, no guilt. Email is the default. A text goes only to someone with documented consent (run the consent check). Never ask about the buyer's personal characteristics, only their reaction to the home.

## Offers and closing

### 4. Present an offer
Follow `references/offers.md`. Deliver:
1. A plain-language summary email under 200 words, sent before the call: what was offered, what it means in practice, and a note that the seller and the member will review it together before any decision.
2. If there are several offers, a side-by-side table.
3. A call opener, two sentences that frame the member as an advisor, not a salesperson.
4. The counter strategy the member suggested: which terms to counter, why, and the likely response, using only what the member gave.
5. If the seller wants to accept an offer the member thinks is weak: what to say, the risks to name, and how to confirm it is an informed choice.
6. The questions for the seller's attorney, CPA, or lender, when terms affect taxes, title, or financing.
Do not calculate or guess net proceeds the member did not provide.

### 5. The final two weeks to closing
Inputs to look for: the closing date, time, and place or remote, the final walkthrough date, open items, possession terms, and what the member is handling. If the CRM holds the contract dates, read them. Dates come from the executed contract: confirm them against it.
Write: 14 days out (email, under 220 words: a timeline, a short checklist with due dates, and what the member handles), 7 days out (text, under 250 characters, only with documented consent, otherwise a short email), 2 days out (email, under 180 words: logistics as confirmed by the title or escrow company, utilities, keys, what to leave, and a reminder never to act on wiring instructions received by email without calling the title company at a known number), closing day (text under 200 characters, with the consent check, or an email), and 3 days after (email under 120 words: thanks, two things to keep such as closing documents and warranty information, and a question about how it went).
Each message refers to a real detail from the inputs and reads like a person wrote it. End emails with: "Confirm every date against the executed document. This is not legal advice."

### 6. A review and referral ask
Inputs to look for: the seller's name, the property, outcome highlights, a specific moment from the transaction, and the review links.
Write: a review request text under 300 characters with the link (consent check first, otherwise email only), a review request email under 150 words (thank them using the specific moment, one sentence for the ask, and 3 prompts such as "what surprised you", "what you'd tell a friend", and "one thing that helped"), a gentle reminder for 5 days later under 200 characters, and a referral ask under 120 words sent after the review or 2 weeks later that names a type of person they might know and tells them exactly what to do.
Do not offer anything of value in exchange for a review or a referral. Do not ask for a specific star rating. Follow the brokerage's referral rules. It must sound like a person thanking a person.

## Rescue

### 7. The case for a price adjustment
Inputs to look for: the current and original price, days on market, showings and offers, feedback themes (filtered), 2 or 3 active competitors and 2 or 3 recent sales (the member provides these), monthly closed sales and active count in the price band, the seller's carrying costs (from the seller), the condition versus the comps, and the member's recommended price or "help me decide". Never estimate numbers that were not given. Say what is needed instead.
**Part 1, the analysis:** where the listing sits (price per square foot versus actives and recent sales, and which home a buyer would choose today and why), the pace (months of supply from the numbers given and what it means), the adjustment (the price that moves the home into the band where buyers are writing offers, with the math shown), the cost of waiting (carrying costs per 30 days only if provided, and how longer time on market tends to affect perception), and a recommended range (a low end that should drive strong activity, a high end that still competes, and where the member would land).
**Part 2, the conversation:** an email under 140 words that plants the seed and asks for a 20 minute call without putting the full ask in writing, the three talking points to say, in order, and answers to "let's wait a little longer", "we can't go that low", and "the right buyer just hasn't come". Keep the tone advisory, not apologetic, and label every assumption.

### 8. Diagnose a stalled listing
Inputs to look for: original and current price, list date and days on market, price reductions, showings, second showings, and offers, feedback themes (filtered), the marketing so far, condition and staging, recent sales and active competition, the seller's mindset, and the member's read on the cause.
**Part 1, the diagnosis (for the member):** rank the top 3 likely causes with the evidence for each, and separate what the data shows from what the member is assuming. Say so if the member's read does not match the evidence. A useful frame is the funnel: exposure, showings, second showings, offers.
**Part 2, the strategy session:** an honest, empathetic summary to say out loud, what buyers are telling the member (the feedback and competition translated into what they are choosing instead), three paths (**A** adjust price with a number and reasoning, **B** improve condition with specific items, **C** withdraw and relaunch with timing), each with what it requires, what it costs, and the likely outcome, the member's recommendation delivered with empathy, and an honest projection of what happens if nothing changes (meant to inform, not scare).
**Part 3, a 14-day action plan** for the recommended path, plus three things not to do at this stage.
**Part 4, a one-page seller report,** printable: header "[Member name] | Listing Review | [Address] | [Date]", then Listing Snapshot, Market Snapshot, What the Data Says, Our Plan (a checklist), and Decision Needed By [Date].
Nothing blames the seller, nothing promises a result, and every number comes from the member.

### 9. A condition improvement plan
For a seller who wants to hold the price when feedback points to condition. Inputs to look for: the current price, the condition feedback (filtered), what the member observed, the seller's budget, the time before relaunch, and any local cost references or vendor quotes.
Deliver: **Tier 1, must do** (items tied to feedback that are costing offers), **Tier 2, should do**, and **Tier 3, skip** (things that feel important to sellers but rarely change offers). For each item give a cost only from a quote or reference the member supplied. If none was supplied, write "get a quote" and do not invent a number. Then the totals for Tier 1 and for Tier 1 plus 2 from the supplied numbers only, an honest return check (do the fixes support the current price, or mainly make the home more competitive at a slightly adjusted price), and a week-by-week timeline that fits the relaunch window, including when photos are retaken. Under 450 words, with a table per tier.
Flag any item that may need permits, a licensed contractor, or an update to the seller's disclosures, and remind the member to have the seller confirm costs with local vendors. This is a plan, not an inspection.

### 10. A withdraw and relaunch plan
Inputs to look for: the current price and days on market, why the member is recommending it, what changes before relaunch, the relaunch date and price, the home's strongest features, and the member's MLS rules on withdrawn versus canceled status and days on market. Scan the profile first. If the rules are not known, tell the member to confirm them with their broker, and never guess.
Honesty rule: never design a withdrawal or relisting to hide days on market or listing history where the MLS rules or law require it to carry over or be disclosed. Withdrawal needs the seller's signed paperwork.
Deliver: a seller script under 200 words that frames withdrawal as a strategy, explains how buyers read long time on market, and why a real change beats grinding it out, then a relaunch timeline week by week (fixes, new photos, coming soon marketing only if `coming_soon_policy` allows it, database and neighbor outreach, launch, first open house), then a pointer to /hi5-re-listing-launch for the relaunch copy (new MLS remarks written from scratch, a social caption, a just-listed email, a neighbor letter), and a talking point under 50 words for agents who ask why it is back. New photos are always required, never reused.

### 11. Prepare for a hard seller conversation
Inputs to look for: the seller's name, the property, the days on market and key numbers (with filtered feedback), what the member needs to tell them, how they will likely react, the decision needed by the end of the call, and any relationship notes.
Deliver: a pre-call email under 150 words (send 24 hours ahead) that acknowledges the stretch has been hard, asks for a set time to talk about the path forward, and keeps a calm, serious tone without giving the full news in writing, the first sentence of the call, the core message under 90 seconds spoken (acknowledge, share the data, recommend, then ask for their view), answers to "we just need the right buyer", "can we wait a bit longer", "I don't want to drop that much", "maybe we need a different agent", and "we'll pull it and try next year", the close (how to land a decision and a next step with a date while the seller feels heard), and a follow-up email under 150 words within 2 hours with the recap, the decision, the next steps with dates, and thanks.
Remove anything that sounds like blame, a guilt trip, or a threat, and keep the recommendation plain.

### 12. Re-earn your expired listing
This job is only for the member's own listing that just expired. Outreach to someone else's expired listing is a different matter, with do-not-call and texting rules, and it is not part of this skill.
Inputs to look for: the original and final price, total days on market, showings, and offers, the member's honest read on why it did not sell, what changed in the market, the seller's state of mind, and what the member would do differently.
Deliver: an expiration call under 200 words, spoken (acknowledge the result, own the member's part where it is fair, share what was learned, and ask for the chance to run a better plan, with no excuses), a relisting proposal under 250 words, written (what was learned, what changes and why, the new price with reasoning tied to current data the member gave, specific commitments such as update cadence and review dates, and a clear next step), one recommended new price with a short reason, relaunch must-haves (new photos, a description written from scratch, an open house within 7 days of going live, neighbor and database outreach, a relaunch of social content), one honest paragraph that rebuilds trust, and a gracious reply for if they go with someone else.
Do not blame the seller or the market for everything. Every commitment must be something the member can deliver, and no result is promised.
