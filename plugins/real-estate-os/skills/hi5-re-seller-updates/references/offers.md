# Offers

Used by `/hi5-re-seller-updates` for job 4 (Present an offer), and wherever an offer, a counter, or an application comes up. Rental listings use applications in place of offers.

## What you do
Summarize and compare, so the seller can decide. The seller decides to accept, counter, or decline. The member advises, and the member's broker and attorney handle the legal side.

## Rules
- **Never calculate or guess net proceeds.** Use only a net sheet or numbers the member provides, and label them an estimate. If none was given, say what is needed.
- **Confidentiality and multiple offers.** Do not share one buyer's terms with another bidder or their agent unless the member's broker, the state rules, and the seller authorize it. Say it depends, and to confirm how to run multiple offers with the broker. Treat every offer fairly and on its terms.
- **Buyer letters.** Personal letters from buyers can carry Fair Housing risk because they can reveal protected details. If one comes up, say so, do not pass on or summarize personal details, and suggest the member follow their broker's policy.
- **Fair Housing.** Compare offers on price, financing, contingencies, timeline, and certainty only. Never weigh a buyer's family, age, religion, origin, or other protected characteristic.
- **Compensation.** Compensation is set by agreement. Never describe a buyer's agent compensation as standard, and follow the MLS and the brokerage's rules on how it is offered and disclosed.
- **No legal or tax advice.** Terms that affect taxes, title, or financing go to the seller's attorney, CPA, or lender, and the draft lists them as questions.
- **Never promise** a result, a price, or a closing.
- For rentals, applications are compared only against the written criteria the owner applies to every applicant.

## What to show
For one offer: price, financing type, down payment, earnest money, contingencies and their timelines, closing date, concessions, and any unusual terms, in plain language, and what each means in practice. For several offers: a side-by-side table of price, estimated net (only if provided), financing strength, contingencies, timeline, and risk. Then the options (accept, counter with terms, decline) and the member's recommendation as the member gave it.
