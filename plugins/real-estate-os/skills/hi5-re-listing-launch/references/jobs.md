# The Ten Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (what the profile, the saved pages, the CRM Map, and the listing details already answer), then ask only for what is missing, and point to what you found. Follow the property type section, run the Fair Housing scan on every draft, and apply `references/safeguards.md`. Drafts only. Every public piece ends with the disclosure line. If something important is still missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

Listing facts to look for before asking: the address or area, the price (or rent), beds, baths, living area, lot, style, year built, the top 3 to 5 specific features (materials, dimensions, orientation), updates with years, nearby amenities with distances (the Neighborhood page often has these), how the home lives (features only), the open house date and time, a property page link, the photos and their status, and the CTA the member likes.

---

## Launch

### 1. MLS remarks and a social version
Deliver:
1. **Three opening lines**, each from a different angle: the single best feature, a moment of daily life shown through the features, or a location advantage. Mark the one you recommend.
2. **MLS remarks**, under 250 words or the member's `mls_remarks_limit`. Lead with the strongest feature, then flow room by room in the order a buyer would walk it, then updates, then location. Every sentence carries a fact.
3. **A social version**, under 150 words, with more personality and one clear call to action (book a showing, DM a keyword, or come to the open house on a date).
4. **One closing sentence** that paints a concrete scene of daily life using light, space, and flow, never the people in it.
Specifics beat adjectives: "quartz island seating four", not "beautiful kitchen". If schools matter, list the assigned school names only if the member gives them, and say to verify with the district. Before finalizing, scan both versions for the banned words, any claim you were not given, and any who-it-suits phrase.

### 2. Five caption angles to test
Five Instagram captions on one listing, each a different angle, labeled: **Curiosity** (a question or an unexpected detail), **Timing** (real urgency from facts only, such as an open house date or low inventory the member can back up, with no pressure language), **Daily life** (a specific moment shown through features), **Numbers** (a stat about the home or the market), and **Micro-story** (something true about the home's history, the renovation, or the street).
Each caption has a hook line under 12 words, a body under 150 characters, and a call to action. Captions 2, 4, and 5 get a direct call to action (a DM keyword, a showing, or the link in bio). Captions 1 and 3 end with a question. Add 3 to 5 relevant local hashtags to each, never generic ones. No two captions open the same way.

### 3. A ten-post content pack
1. Instagram caption, daily-life angle (under 150 words)
2. Instagram caption, market or numbers angle (under 120 words)
3. Facebook post, longer and neighborhood-focused (150 to 220 words)
4. LinkedIn post for the member's professional network: what makes this listing notable in the local market (under 150 words)
5. Just-listed sphere email: a subject line and a body under 120 words, written like a personal note
6. Reel: 3 hook options for the first 3 seconds and a 6-shot list
7. Story sequence: 3 frames, including one poll or quiz sticker idea
8. Blog post intro (120 words) and an H2 outline for a neighborhood-focused article
9. Google Business Profile update (under 100 words, with a call to action button suggestion)
10. A text to a buyer whose saved search matches, under 300 characters, with an easy opt-out line. Draft it only for a buyer with documented consent. If consent is unknown, draft an email instead.
Use a different hook for each piece and rotate features. Each piece ends with one call to action suited to its platform and carries the disclosure line where public posts require it. Deliver as a numbered list with the platform as each heading.

### 4. A just-listed email for the sphere
Give 3 subject lines under 45 characters (one curiosity, one plain, one location-led, no all caps and no exclamation marks), preview text under 90 characters, and a body under 120 words. Open like a note to a friend, give three highlights as concrete facts, mention the open house, and end with one specific ask: "If someone comes to mind who has been looking in [area], forward this or reply with their name and I'll reach out personally." Add a P.S. with one more detail, and the disclosure line (`disclosure_line_full`) at the bottom. Before finalizing, read it as a recipient: if the first two lines sound like a mass blast, rewrite them.
The member sends it from their own email tool to people who have agreed to receive it, with the normal unsubscribe line. Offer numbered steps for their tool if they want them.

### 5. The graphics brief (and an optional build in Canva)
Always write the brief. For each of these formats, give the headline (6 words at most), the supporting lines, the photo placeholder note, and the call to action: an Instagram feed post (1080 by 1350), a Story (1080 by 1920, with the top third kept clear for a link sticker), a Facebook post (1200 by 630), a printable flyer (8.5 by 11 with a QR code placeholder for the property page), and an email header (600 by 200).
Rules: one headline per design, price and beds and baths always visible, brand colors and fonts from the profile (`brand_color`, `brand_font`) or the member's Canva brand kit, minimal text over photos with strong contrast, the brokerage name and required disclosure on every design at a readable size, and everything editable. Then write the Instagram caption, the Facebook caption, and a short just-listed email subject and body.
**Optional Canva build.** If the Canva connection is available in this session and the member wants it, show the planned headline and text for each design and wait for their OK. After the OK, create them as drafts in the member's own Canva account and give the link for each. Never publish, share, or export without a further OK. Use only photos the member supplies. Say the Fair Housing scan covered the text before anything is built.

### 6. A 30-day marketing calendar
Cover from 7 days before the list date through day 30. Scan the listing details first, then ask only about what is unknown (the list date, the seller's main goal, the assets the member has, which channels they use, and honest challenges with the property).
For each task give the day, the channel, the exact content or action (the actual hook or message, not "post on Instagram"), the time it takes, and why it matters at this stage.
- **Pre-list (days -7, -5, -3, -1):** prep, coming soon teasers only if `coming_soon_policy` allows them, neighbor outreach, and an agent network heads-up.
- **Week 1 launch (days 1 to 7):** a task every day, day 1 the biggest, an open house on the first weekend.
- **Week 2 momentum (8 to 14):** sustained visibility and showing follow-up.
- **Week 3 review (15 to 21):** a mid-campaign check on showings, feedback, saves, and online views, with a clear decision to hold, adjust the marketing, or discuss price.
- **Week 4 re-engage (22 to 30):** re-contact every interested buyer and agent, and re-launch the content with a fresh angle.
- **Week 5 re-strategy (only if not under contract):** a photo refresh, pricing conversation prep, and marketing reset options.
Rules: rotate highlights so the same feature never leads two days in a row, at least 3 tasks turn a challenge into an honest selling angle without hiding it, at least 2 neighbor outreach tasks in week 1, and one database email each week. Flag any task that depends on showing feedback. Any paid ad task says: no age, gender, or ZIP targeting, and a radius of about 15 miles or more, and points to /hi5-re-ads (coming) or /hi5-funnel for the setup.
Finish with the 3 tasks that matter most if the member can only do three, a challenge playbook (the angle, the wording, and what to disclose for each challenge), a content bank of 10 extra post ideas with hooks, and a checklist version they can print or paste.
**Hand-offs.** Offer to create the CRM tasks with /hi5-re-crm, and calendar events if a calendar connection is available. Show the plan first and wait for the member's OK. Keep event titles free of sensitive details.

### 7. An open house kit
**Before:** neighbor invite (door hanger or postcard copy, under 60 words, inviting neighbors to see the home first and share it with anyone they know who is looking), a social post and a Story frame, and a reminder text under 200 characters for people who RSVP (consent check first).
**Day of:** sign-in card wording that says why contact info is asked and how it will be used, with a line about texts needing the visitor's agreement (for the member's broker to review), and three conversation openers that uncover timeline and whether the visitor has a home to sell.
**After:** a same-day thank-you text under 250 characters with one question, a day-2 email with a feedback request and a placeholder for 2 similar active homes, a day-7 check-in for visitors who also need to sell (offering a quick value range), and a feedback summary the member can send the seller the next morning.
Follow-ups go only to visitors who gave contact info and agreed to be contacted. Never use phrasing that invites people to choose their neighbors. To log visitors, use /hi5-re-crm job 3 after the member's OK.

## Milestones

### 8. A just-sold story
Confirm the seller approved sharing, and read `sold_price_policy` (see safeguards). If the seller did not approve personal details, keep it to the property and the numbers the member may share. Use numbers the member gave, never "sold in record time" without the record.
1. **A social post under 100 words:** open with the seller's goal, show the one thing that made the difference, close with the result. Give 2 hook options.
2. **An email to the database, 150 to 200 words:** the same story with one practical takeaway for homeowners in the area, and a soft call to action ("If you're curious what your home would do in this market, reply and I'll send you a quick range").
3. **A one-line caption** for a sold-sign photo.
The seller is the hero and the member is the guide, with at most one sentence about the member. No personal details about the buyers. Add the disclosure line. Remove any line that reads as bragging.

### 9. An under contract post
1. **A social post under 80 words:** celebrate the milestone and thank the clients (by name only with permission), then turn to the buyers who missed it: "If this one was on your list, send me what you loved about it and I'll watch for the next one."
2. **A Story version under 25 words** with a "DM me [keyword]" call to action.
3. **A two sentence reply template** for people who comment or ask about similar homes.
Say "under contract" or "pending", never "sold". Share no price and no terms. One celebration line, and the rest is useful. Promise off-market homes only if the member has access to them, otherwise say "as soon as they come up".

### 10. A price improvement announcement
1. **An email under 80 words** with 2 subject lines, leading with the value the new price unlocks, not the drop.
2. **A social version under 80 words** with a direct call to action.
3. **A text under 280 characters** for buyers and agents who showed it before. Texts go only to people with documented consent, and an email goes to everyone else.
Use "new price" or "price improvement". Reveal nothing about the seller's reasons or situation.
