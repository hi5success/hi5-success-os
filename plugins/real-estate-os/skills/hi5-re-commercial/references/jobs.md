# The Seven Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (the CRM record, the profile, earlier Marketing Hub rows for this client or property, and the documents the member gave), ask only about what could not be found, and point to what you found. Figures come only from the documents and the client. Attorney review is a step in every phase. Drafts only. If something important is missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

---

## Start

### 1. The requirements intake
Inputs to look for: whether the client is a tenant, a buyer, an owner, or a landlord, the use, the size, the location criteria in property terms (an address they name, a radius, a named submarket they chose), the budget as the client states it, the timeline and any date that drives it (a lease expiration the client gives), who decides, and any must-haves and deal-breakers (parking, loading, power, signage, access, as features). Read the CRM first and ask only about what is missing.
Build a one-page Requirements Brief with "Not yet known" and the question to ask for each gap, and the next step. Offer to log it with /hi5-re-crm after the member's OK.

### 2. The total occupancy cost comparison
Build a worksheet for two to five spaces. For each, the client or a document supplies: base rent and its unit as written, additional charges and how they are billed, the term, any free rent or allowance stated in the document, build-out costs from quotes, parking, utilities, and any other cost. Compute only what the inputs support: the cost per period over the term as the documents state it, with every step shown and the source of each number. Mark missing inputs and say which totals cannot be calculated yet. Never state or estimate a market rate. Never choose the best option. Add: "Have your attorney confirm how each charge is defined in the lease."

### 3. The letter of intent outline
Build an outline with section headings (parties, the premises or property, term, rent or price, additional charges, improvements and allowances, options, deposit, contingencies and due diligence, closing or commencement, confidentiality, and the signatures), and under each, the client's stated position in their own words, or "[client and attorney to complete]". Never fill in a term the client did not state, and never say what is customary. End with: "This is an outline for you and your attorney. It is not a legal document. Your attorney reviews and finalizes any letter of intent."

## Represent

### 4. Tenant rep and landlord rep talk tracks
Build, for the role the member plays: an opening that explains what the member does and how the member is paid, with compensation set by agreement and negotiable (use only the member's saved structure and never quote a standard rate), the discovery questions, the objection answers from the member's Objection Bank (marked [ADD PROOF] if generic), and a plain-English explanation of the process steps without stating legal terms. Include a follow-up email after the first meeting (under 150 words) and the question "who else decides and when?".

---

## Check and run the deal

### 5. The due diligence and lease review checklist
Build a checklist by phase (letter of intent, lease or contract, due diligence, financing if any, title, closing or commencement), each item with an owner (the client, the member, the landlord or seller, the attorney, a lender, an inspector, an environmental professional, a surveyor, or title) and a due date from the document or "confirm with document". Include: financial statements, the rent roll and leases, estoppels and subordination items, operating expenses and billing, the survey and title, zoning and permitted use (as a question for the planning office), environmental, building condition, insurance, the signage and parking rights, assignment and subletting, renewal and termination terms, personal guarantees and deposits (for the attorney), and a task "attorney review" in every phase. For each clause the member reads, quote it with its page, and put the meaning on the attorney's list.

### 6. The deal tracker and weekly client update
Build a tracker table (the phase, the item, the owner, the date, the status) and a weekly update email under 150 words: a one-line status, what was done, what is next with dates from the documents, what the client needs to decide, and one calm closing sentence. For the key dates calendar, point to /hi5-re-transaction (the commercial file type) and /hi5-re-crm job 5 for the tasks, after the member's OK.

### 7. Advertising notes for a commercial listing
Say that commercial advertising is treated as Housing unless the member's broker confirms otherwise, give the Meta Housing limits from /hi5-re-ads (no age, gender, or ZIP targeting, a radius of at least 15 miles, no lookalikes), and point to /hi5-re-ads for the pack and /hi5-re-listing-launch for listing copy that uses the offering documents only. Check Meta's current policy before you launch.
