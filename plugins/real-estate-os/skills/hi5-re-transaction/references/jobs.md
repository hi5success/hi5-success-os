# The Ten Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (the CRM opportunity and its fields, the profile, earlier Marketing Hub rows for this address, and the document), ask only about what could not be found, and point to what you found. Dates come only from the executed document. Drafts only. Every message ends with the disclosure line where it is public or goes to a client by email, and every message sent before closing day that is about closing carries the wire fraud reminder: never act on wiring instructions received by email without calling the title company at a known number. If something important is still missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

---

## Start

### 1. Read the contract or lease
Follow `../hi5-re-crm/references/documents.md` exactly: name the document type and the side the member represents, extract every party, date, and term with the page or section it came from, work out a date only where the document states the rule and show the math, and put anything unclear on a "Needs confirmation" list with the paragraph to re-read. Show the result as one table (Item, Value, Where it came from) and the Needs confirmation list. Leave out sensitive data and say so. Offer the checklist (job 2) and the calendar (job 3) next, and offer to put the extracted fields in the CRM with /hi5-re-crm job 5 after the member's OK.

### 2. The file checklist
Use the phases for the file type in `by-file-type.md`. Build one table per phase with the columns Task, Owner, Due date, and Done [ ]. Dates come only from the executed document. If a date is not in the document or the CRM, write "confirm with contract". Mark the 5 items most likely to slip on this side of the file with [!] and add one line on how to prevent each. Then write a short "Your job this week" list in plain English that the member can paste into a text to the client. Add the line: "Reminder: this is a working checklist, not legal advice. Your state contract, local addenda, and broker's requirements control every deadline and task. Confirm dates against the executed contract."

### 3. The key dates calendar
Use the extraction from job 1 (or the CRM fields). Ask only whether deadlines count business or calendar days if the document and the CRM do not say. If it is unknown, do not compute the dates that depend on it: show the clause and write "confirm" in the date column until the member answers. Build:
1. A table: Deadline, Date, Time (if stated), What must happen, Who acts, Reminder 3 days before, Reminder 1 day before, and a 48 hour warning.
2. A "Needs confirmation" list with the paragraph to re-read.
3. Draft event titles in the form [Address], [Deadline], [Owner].
4. The hand-off. Show the full list of CRM tasks and calendar events as a plan and wait for the member's OK before creating anything. Create the CRM tasks through /hi5-re-crm (job 5) and the calendar events through the calendar connection if it is available. Make them in batches, read each back, and report any that failed. Keep event titles free of sensitive details.

## Keep everyone informed

### 4. The weekly client status update
Inputs to look for: the client's name, which side, the property, the phase, what was done this week, the next milestone and its date, the client's action items, and anything uncertain or delayed. The CRM tasks and notes often answer most of this.
Write: 2 subject lines under 50 characters that include the address or street, an email under 150 words in this order (a one line status, done this week, coming next, their to-dos as bullets with due dates, and one calm closing sentence about what the member is watching for them), and a 2 sentence text version, only if the consent check passes.
Rules: no jargon without a 5 word plain English explanation. If something is delayed, say so directly and give the plan. Do not promise anything outside the member's control (appraisal value, underwriting, repairs). For a tenant or landlord file, report the lease steps instead.
If the member wants it saved in Gmail, save a draft only after their OK, never send.

### 5. Offer communications
**Acknowledge an offer on your listing.** Write a short email to the buyer's agent (under 100 words) that confirms receipt, says the seller will review it with the member, and sets a response window. Use only the window the member or the seller gave. Do not share terms with anyone else. Add a plain summary of the key terms for the member's own file (price, financing, earnest money, contingencies, closing date).
**A buyer-side cover letter.** Write a cover note to the listing agent under 150 words that makes the offer easy to say yes to by stating certainty facts the member gave: the financing type and strength, the earnest money, the timeline, and flexibility on possession or inspection. Facts only. Never include personal details or a personal letter about the buyers.
To present and compare offers to a seller, point to /hi5-re-seller-updates (job 4).

## Negotiate

### 6. An inspection repair request or response
Inputs to look for: which side, the property, the items in play from the report or the request, contractor quotes if any, the client's priorities and limits, and the deadline. Quote the inspector. Never diagnose a condition.
Deliver:
1. The items sorted into safety or major systems, items a lender or insurer is likely to flag, and minor or cosmetic items, with a note on anything that needs a licensed professional to evaluate.
2. A recommended ask (buyer) or response (seller) per item, repairs versus a credit versus a price adjustment, with a one line reason, for the client to approve.
3. A cover note to the other agent under 150 words, collaborative, referencing the report, listing what is asked or offered, and stating the deadline.
4. A 3 sentence explanation for the client of why this approach protects them.
Credits and repairs follow the member's state contract forms and the lender's rules. Follow the broker's process. Not legal advice.

### 7. An appraisal gap brief
Inputs to look for: which side, the client's name, the contract price, the appraised value and the gap, the appraisal contingency or gap clause as the document states it, the financing type and down payment, the options the member sees, and any comps the appraiser may have missed.
Deliver:
1. A client email under 200 words: what happened in one sentence, what it means for them, the options as a short numbered list with the cash or timing effect of each, and the decision deadline.
2. A prep table for the member: Option, What it costs the client, Risk, Deadline, and Who needs to act.
3. Talking points for a 10 minute call, including 3 questions that help the client choose.
4. If a reconsideration of value makes sense, an outline of what to send the lender, using only comps the member supplies.
Be calm and factual with no blame on the appraiser. Never propose a compromise number or a split that the member did not give. Present only the options the member listed, with the numbers the member supplied. Never describe a value in terms of the neighborhood's character or the people in it. Present trade-offs and never choose for the client. Loan impact goes to the lender, and termination rights go to the contract and the member's broker.

## Close

### 8. The final walkthrough sheet
Build a phone-friendly sheet for the client:
- **Buyer or new construction:** a "bring with you" list (the repair amendment, receipts, a phone charger), a checklist by room with checkboxes (what to test and what "good" looks like), the agreed repairs with how to verify each and the receipt or warranty to collect, and an "if something isn't right" section (document it with photos, tell the member before leaving, and the remedy depends on the contract and the broker's guidance). For new construction, use the builder's punch list.
- **Tenant:** a move-in condition report to record the condition of each room with photos and dates.
- **Landlord:** a move-out condition checklist.
Short lines, no paragraph over 2 sentences. Not legal advice. Follow the state contract for walkthrough rights and timing.

### 9. The closing day message
Write a personal congratulations text or email and the card and closing gift note. Use the client's own details from the CRM and notes (only what the member or the client shared), never protected or sensitive details. For a lease, write the keys and welcome message. If the member gives you a settlement statement, use only names, the property, and the date, and never copy any account or bank numbers. Include the wire fraud reminder in anything sent before closing. Texts go only with documented consent.
Offer to close the deal in the CRM with /hi5-re-crm job 7 after the member's OK.

### 10. A message after a deal falls apart
Write a short message to the client: acknowledge it, take the pressure off, and offer one clear next step. No blame on the client, the other side, or the lender. Do not say who is entitled to the earnest money, because the contract and the broker decide. Offer to log the lost deal and plan the comeback with /hi5-re-crm job 8. Texts go only with documented consent.
