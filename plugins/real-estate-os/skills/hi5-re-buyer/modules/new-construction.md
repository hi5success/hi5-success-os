# Module: New Construction Buyer

A buyer considering a home from a builder, built or to be built.

## Discovery questions (skip anything the scan answered)
1. Which builders and communities are you looking at, and have you visited any?
2. Is it a finished home, one under construction, or one to be built? What is your move-in date?
3. Which options and upgrades matter most to you, and what is your budget for them?
4. How will you pay, and is the builder's preferred lender part of an incentive you are considering?
5. Do you want an independent inspector at key stages?

## Notes
- Have the member's representation set up with the builder before the buyer's first visit when possible. Say this is how the member works and that the broker confirms the builder's rules on representation. Never state a legal rule.
- Builder contract: the member reads the contract with the buyer and lists the questions to ask the builder, such as deposits and what they cover, the selections deadlines, who decides substitutions, how completion dates and delays are handled, what the warranty covers, and what happens to deposits if the buyer cancels. The buyer should consider attorney review. The member never interprets the contract as a lawyer.
- Completion dates are the builder's estimates, never promises. Put "estimated" on every date.
- Incentives (for example a lender incentive or closing cost help): compare them using the numbers in the builder's documents and the lender's estimate. Never state their value from memory.
- Inspection plan: independent inspections at stages the contract allows (for example before drywall and at the final), and the final walkthrough with a punch list. Point to /hi5-re-transaction (the new construction notes).
- Comparisons between communities (see the vault's new construction comparison) use the builders' own materials and facts the member gathers. Never describe a community by who lives there.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "The builder's agent can help me, so why do I need my own?"
2. "Is the upgrade price fair?" Compare with quotes the buyer or the member provides.
3. The member's Objection Bank answers.

## Follow-up notes
Keep a running list of selections with deadlines, and send the buyer a short reminder before each.
