# Module: Renter With Tenant Representation

Someone looking to rent, where the member represents the tenant. The landlord side is a separate plan, coming in /hi5-re-rental.

## Discovery questions (skip anything the scan answered)
1. When do you need to move in, and when does your current lease end (if you have one)?
2. What is your monthly budget for rent, and what other monthly costs do you want counted (utilities, parking, pet fees)? The tenant supplies the numbers.
3. What must the place have (bedrooms, laundry, parking, accessibility features, a pet policy that fits)? What is a deal-breaker?
4. Which areas or addresses matter to you (a commute to an address you name)?
5. What do you need to apply (documents the landlord's written criteria list), and do you want help gathering them?

## Notes
- Do not record household details. Record property needs only, such as the number of bedrooms and the features. Never ask about or record who will live in the home beyond what is needed to put a name on a lease application, and let the tenant complete the landlord's own application.
- Screening: landlords apply their written criteria the same way to every applicant. The member tells the tenant to ask for the written criteria, and never suggests who is more or less likely to be approved by anything other than what the written criteria say.
- Move-in cost sheet: list the categories the landlord's documents state (first month, deposit, fees, pet deposit, utilities setup) with the amounts from the listing or lease, and "confirm with landlord" where they are not shown. Never state amounts or rules from memory.
- Lease review checklist: term and renewal, rent and when it can change, deposit and how it is returned, repairs and who handles them, early termination, subletting, pets, parking, and access. The lease is a legal document, so suggest attorney review, and the member never gives legal advice or states what a state's law provides.
- Move-in condition report: photos and notes of each room with dates, delivered to the landlord within the lease's timeline. Point to /hi5-re-transaction (the tenant lease notes and walkthrough sheet).
- Compensation: tenant representation can be paid in different ways, set by agreement and negotiable. Use only the member's saved structure, state it plainly if the buyer asks, and never quote a standard amount.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "I can find a rental myself."
2. "Do I need to pay you?" Explain how the member is paid under the member's agreement, using only the member's saved structure.
3. "Will I get approved?" The landlord's written criteria decide.
4. The member's Objection Bank answers for renters.

## Follow-up notes
Send a short checklist after each tour (the place, the numbers the listing gives, the questions to ask the landlord), and keep dates from the lease.
