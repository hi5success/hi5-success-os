# Module: Land Buyer

A buyer looking at vacant land, acreage, or a lot.

## Discovery questions (skip anything the scan answered)
1. What do you want to do with the land (build, hold, farm, recreation, or something else)?
2. How much land and what features do you need (frontage, access, water, utilities, topography)?
3. What is your timeline, and how are you paying (land loans can differ from home loans, so ask the lender)?
4. Which professionals do you already have (surveyor, civil engineer, attorney, builder, lender)?

## Due diligence checklist (for qualified professionals)
Survey and boundaries, legal access and easements, utilities available at the lot line and what connection costs and requires, water and septic or sewer, soil and percolation tests where relevant, flood and drainage, zoning and permitted uses, deed restrictions and any HOA rules, taxes and any special districts, mineral and water rights, environmental concerns, and the title commitment. Each item has an owner (the buyer, a professional, the title company, or the member) and a due date from the contract or "confirm with contract".

## Notes
- Never guess zoning or what can be built. Say the local planning office and the buyer's professionals answer that, and give the question to ask them.
- Never state a value, a cost to build, a cost to connect utilities, or a timeline for permits. Those come from professionals' quotes.
- Land is often contingent on due diligence. Say the contract controls the contingency periods and the member confirms them with the broker.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "How do I know I can build on it?"
2. "It's cheap per acre." Say price per acre alone does not show what can be done, and point to the due diligence.
3. The member's Objection Bank answers.

## Follow-up notes
Send the checklist with owners and dates after the consultation, and update it at each step.
