# Module: Investor Buyer

Someone buying a property to rent, flip, or hold. Treat the investor's numbers as the investor's.

## Discovery questions (skip anything the scan answered)
1. What is the plan for the property (rent, flip, hold, or a mix) and what is your timeline?
2. What is your buy box: price range, property types, unit counts, condition, and areas you chose?
3. What numbers do you use to compare properties? (Rent, expenses, vacancy, financing terms, and any rule of thumb. The investor supplies them. Never supply them.)
4. How are you financing it, and have you talked to a lender who works with investors?
5. Do you have a property manager, attorney, and CPA, or would you like referrals from the member's network?

## Analysis notes
- Compare properties only on the factors and numbers the investor gave, and show the math step by step. Label every assumption and say the investor owns it.
- Never state or promise a return, a yield, appreciation, rent, or "a good deal". Never state market rent. If the investor wants a rent estimate, say a property manager or a rental comp report the member provides is the source.
- If the property is occupied, due diligence includes the leases, the rent roll, tenant notices, and estoppel steps as the documents require, with attorney review. See /hi5-re-transaction (the investor purchase notes).
- Never choose areas or properties by the people who live there.
- Tax, 1031 exchange, and entity questions go to the investor's CPA and attorney.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "I only want the deals that pencil out." Ask for their numbers and run the math they define.
2. "Can you find me off-market deals?" Describe what the member actually does, and never promise supply.
3. The member's Objection Bank answers for investors.

## Follow-up notes
Send a short scorecard after each property: the investor's own metrics side by side, with the source of each number.
