# The Twelve Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (the CRM record and notes, the profile, the CRM Map, earlier Marketing Hub rows for this buyer, the buyer-type file in `../modules/`, and anything the member pasted), ask only about what could not be found, and point to what you found. Needs are property needs only. Every touch passes the gate in `../../hi5-re-sphere/references/touch-rules.md`. Every number comes from the buyer, the member, or a document. Drafts only. Emails end with the disclosure line. If something important is still missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

---

## Start

### 1. The buyer consultation game plan
Inputs to look for: the buyer's first name, how they came to the member, the buyer type, the format (in person, video, or phone), the length of the meeting, and anything already known from the CRM.
Build:
1. **A one-line goal** for the meeting: leave with a clear Buyer Brief and a next step booked.
2. **An agenda with times** that fits the meeting length: welcome and what to expect, their story in their words, the process from search to keys in plain English (the steps the member actually follows), what they want in a property, financing and timeline, how the member works with them, the agreement conversation (job 3) when the member wants it in this meeting, and the next step.
3. **Questions in order,** skipping anything the scan already answered: what are you trying to do and why now, when do you need to be in, how are you planning to pay (cash, loan, or not sure, and have you talked to a lender), what must the home have, what would be nice, what would be a deal-breaker, what have you already looked at and what did you like or not, how will you decide, who else is part of the decision, and what would make this process a good one for you. Never ask about family makeup, age, health, religion, origin, or income beyond what the lender needs. If a buyer volunteers such details, listen, and record only the property need behind them.
4. **A plain-English "how this will go" one-pager for the buyer** (under 150 words) the member can send before or after: the steps, what the buyer does at each, and when to expect to hear from the member.
5. **A short close:** the next step, the date, and one thing for the buyer to do before then.
Adapt the questions and the one-pager to the buyer type from `../modules/`. Offer the Buyer Brief (job 2) next.

### 2. Needs analysis: the one-page Buyer Brief
Inputs to look for: the CRM record, the notes from the consultation, and what the member pastes.
Build a one-page Buyer Brief with these sections, using "Not yet known" for anything missing:
- **The goal and the timeline** in the buyer's words, and any date that drives it (a lease end, a job start, a school year the buyer named, a closing on a sale).
- **Budget and financing** as the buyer stated them, with the source (for example "pre-approval letter dated [date] for [amount]" only if the buyer gave it). Never calculate a payment, a loan amount, or what they can afford.
- **Must-haves, nice-to-haves, and deal-breakers,** property features only: beds and baths, size, lot, layout, accessibility features, parking, outdoor space, condition and how much work they will take on, a commute to an address the buyer named, and the property types they will consider.
- **Where,** as areas the buyer chose (city, neighborhood names they gave, a radius around an address), never as a description of the people.
- **Conflicts and trade-offs,** stated neutrally and in terms of the property (for example "the budget and the three bedrooms near the address you named may not both be possible, and here are the trade-offs to talk about"), never a ruling on what they can have.
- **What is still unknown,** with the question to ask for each.
- **What I will do next,** and what the buyer will do next.
Never include anything about who the buyer is or who lives where. Offer to log the fields with /hi5-re-crm (job 3 for a new contact, job 5 or the note job for details) after the member's OK, and the search plan (job 4) next.

### 3. The buyer agreement conversation
This job explains the member's own agreement and prepares the conversation. It never writes the agreement's terms and never gives legal advice.
Inputs to look for: whether the member's brokerage requires a written agreement before touring or making an offer (ask the member, never state it as law), the member's own form (the member pastes it, or you use only what they tell you), the member's saved fee details, and the buyer's questions.
Build:
1. **A plain-English walk through the member's own form,** section by section as the member pastes it: what the member does for the buyer, what the buyer agrees to, the length and how it can end, and how the member is paid. Quote the form and say "your broker's form controls" and "the buyer may want to have an attorney review it".
2. **The compensation talk track.** Say plainly that compensation is set by agreement between the member and the buyer, that it is negotiable, and that it can be structured in different ways. Use only the amounts and structure the member gives you or has saved. Never state a standard, typical, or market rate, and never say a fee is set by law or a board. If the member has no saved structure, ask for what they use and do not invent one.
3. **Answers to the questions buyers ask,** each under 60 words and honest: "Why do I need to sign this?", "What if I find a house myself?", "Can you do it for less?", "What if I'm not happy with you?", and "Who pays you?". Each answer points to the member's own form and the broker for the details. No pressure and no guarantee, and never imply the buyer cannot tour without signing unless the member tells you that is their broker's rule.
4. **A short practice script** for the member (the buyer's question, the member's answer, and the follow-up question).
5. **A follow-up email** (under 120 words) that thanks them, lists what was agreed, and attaches the next step. Never state compensation numbers in an email unless the member gives the text.

### 4. The search plan
Inputs to look for: the Buyer Brief, the MLS the member uses (`mls_name`), and how often the buyer wants to hear from the member.
Build:
1. **The search criteria** as a list of property filters taken from the Buyer Brief (price range, beds, baths, size, property type, features, areas as chosen by the buyer). Never add or remove an area by who lives there.
2. **How the alerts will run:** the steps for the member to set up saved searches in their own MLS portal (using the member's portal terms only if the member names them), how often matches go to the buyer, and what the buyer does with each (love it, maybe, no, and why in property terms).
3. **A "how we'll search" note for the buyer** (under 120 words): how often they will hear from the member, what to send back, and how the member will adjust the search after the first tours.
4. **A first-week plan:** the first 3 actions for the member and the first 2 for the buyer.
For on-screen MLS matching and drafting a buyer alert email from listings, point to /hi5-re-daily job 10. Never use a browser tool here.

### 5. A showing plan
Inputs to look for: the properties the buyer wants to see (addresses the member gives), the day and available times, the buyer's must-haves, and any access instructions from the listings.
Build:
1. **A route and schedule** in a sensible order with the member's times (the member confirms each showing time with the listing side). Leave time between stops, and say plainly when a time depends on a confirmation.
2. **A one-page tour sheet for the buyer:** for each property, the address, the time, the key features from the listing, and 3 questions to look for on site (property-focused: condition, layout against the must-haves, and anything the listing did not say). Never describe the neighborhood's people or character.
3. **A feedback form** with the same questions for every property: what did you like, what concerns you, how does it compare to your must-haves, and does it move up or down your list. About the property only.
4. **Showing day messages** (drafts): a confirmation to the buyer the day before (email, or a text only if the gate passes) and a short note for each listing agent if the member wants one.
5. **A safety note for the member:** share the schedule with someone in the office, check the buyer's identity per the brokerage's policy, and follow the brokerage's showing rules.
Offer to create the calendar events after the member sees the plan and says OK.

### 6. After-showing follow-up
Inputs to look for: which properties they saw, the buyer's reactions (in the buyer's words), and the buyer's current ranking.
Build:
1. **A debrief script** the member can use in person or by phone: 5 questions that rank the properties against the Buyer Brief and update the must-haves ("What surprised you?", "Is there anything you would change on the list?").
2. **A follow-up email** (under 120 words) that thanks them, lists what was liked and what concerned them in the buyer's words, and gives the next step (another tour, an offer discussion, or a search adjustment). A text version only if the gate passes.
3. **The updated Buyer Brief changes** as a short list (add, remove, or change), shown for the member's OK.
4. **CRM log:** offer to log the showings and the next step with /hi5-re-crm (job 4) after the member's OK. Never record steering remarks the buyer or a listing agent made.

## Offers

### 7. Offer strategy briefing
Inputs to look for: the property and its listing details (the member gives them), the member's comps or CMA, the buyer's priorities and limits in the buyer's words, the financing type and the pre-approval details the buyer gave, the days on market and any price history the member provides, and whether the member knows of other offers.
Build a briefing for the member to use with the buyer:
1. **The facts:** list price, days on market, price changes, and the comps the member provides, each marked as the member's data. Never state a value, and say when comps are thin. Never invent a comp or a trend.
2. **The buyer's priorities** in order, from the Buyer Brief.
3. **The levers,** each with a plain explanation of what it does for the buyer and what it risks: price, earnest money, financing terms, inspection and other contingencies, appraisal terms, closing date and possession, and anything else the seller listed. Say the risks of waiving anything and that waiving a protection is the buyer's decision after the member explains the risk. Never advise waiving anything.
4. **Two or three option packages** (for example "stronger on price", "stronger on certainty", "balanced") using only numbers the member or the buyer provides. Show the trade-offs and never pick one for the buyer.
5. **Questions to ask the listing agent** before the offer, about the seller's priorities and timing, and about anything that affects the buyer's terms.
6. **A decision sheet** with the buyer's own limit written by the buyer, which the member never overrides.
Never guarantee acceptance. Never state what a state's law or the standard form provides. Loan and lending impact goes to the lender, and contract terms go to the member's broker and the buyer's attorney if they want one. For the cover letter, point to /hi5-re-transaction (job 5).

### 8. Multiple-offer prep
Inputs to look for: the property, what the member knows about the offers (only what the listing agent has said and what the rules allow), the deadline for offers, and the buyer's limits.
Build:
1. **How a multiple-offer situation works in general,** in plain English, with the member's note that practice varies and the member will confirm what applies. Never state a state's rule.
2. **What the buyer controls and does not control.**
3. **The decision sheet** (job 7) with the buyer's own limit, the terms they might strengthen and the ones they will not move, and a plan for what to do if the offer is not chosen.
4. **A script** for the member to talk the buyer through the decision calmly, with no pressure, no fake urgency, and no "you must" language. Include the sentence "this is your decision and I will support either choice".
5. **A caution on personal letters.** Say that a letter from a buyer about their family or personal story can create Fair Housing risk for the seller and the agents, and that the member should check their broker's policy. Do not write a personal story about the buyer. Offer only a facts-based cover note through /hi5-re-transaction (job 5).
6. **The next steps after the result,** a win or a loss, with a short message for each (email, or a text only if the gate passes).
Never suggest an escalation clause, a number above the buyer's limit, or a waiver unless the member gives it, and even then lay out the risks and let the buyer decide.

### 9. What happens after the offer
Inputs to look for: the stage of the offer (submitted, countered, or accepted) and the buyer's financing type.
Build a one-page "what happens next" for the buyer (under 200 words, plain English) that lists the typical steps for this stage in the order the member's contract uses, what the buyer does at each, and a note that every date will come from the executed contract. Add the line: "Confirm every date against the executed document. This is not legal advice." Then point to /hi5-re-transaction for the checklist, the key dates calendar, and the weekly client update, and say that /hi5-re-crm job 5 can put the contract into the CRM.

## Keep buyers moving

### 10. The weekly buyer update
Inputs to look for: what the member did this week (searches run, showings, calls with listing agents, lender steps), what is new on the market against the Buyer Brief (only listings the member gives you), what is next, and anything the buyer needs to decide. The CRM tasks and notes often answer most of this.
Write: 2 subject lines under 50 characters, an email under 150 words in this order (a one-line status, done this week, coming next, the buyer's to-dos with dates, and one calm closing sentence), and a 2-sentence text version only if the gate passes. If it was a slow week, say so directly and say what the member is doing about it. Never blame the market, the buyer, or other agents, and never state a market trend or a number the member did not give. Offer to log it with /hi5-re-crm (job 4) after the member's OK.

### 11. Nurture for a buyer who is not ready yet
Inputs to look for: why they are not ready, in the buyer's own words (credit, savings, a lease end, a job or school-year change, rates, selling first, or just exploring), the buyer's target timeline, the CRM tags, and the touches already sent.
Build a 6-month plan:
1. **A segment line** based only on what the buyer said (for example "ready when the lease ends in March", "building savings", "waiting on rates").
2. **A rhythm table:** Month, Channel, Purpose, and the message written out (under 80 words each). Purposes include a helpful guide or resource the member has, a "what would change your plan" question, a re-qualify check at the member's chosen interval (for example "has anything changed with your timeline?"), a lender introduction (only when the buyer asked for one, and the member names the lender), and an invitation to a buyer education session if the member runs one. Never use fake urgency, and never pitch in a first touch.
3. **Channel rules:** email by default. Texts and calls only for people who pass the gate. Never put a rate number or a prediction in a message. For "waiting on rates", say the member can explain how a payment is built and the lender can run the actual numbers.
4. **Triggers that move the buyer into the search:** the buyer says the timeline moved up, a lender letter arrives, or a lease end gets close. Say what the member does at each.
5. **Opt-out:** every email carries the member's unsubscribe line, and any "stop" is honored with the opt-out rule in `touch-rules.md`.
Offer to build the plan as a workflow outline for /hi5-re-crm (job 10) after the member's OK.

### 12. "I want to wait" conversations
Inputs to look for: the reason the buyer gave and the member's Objection Bank answers for buyers.
Build, for the specific reason (rates, a market they think will drop, prices, a sale they are waiting on, or "I'm just looking"): the concern behind the words, one clarifying question, a calm response under 80 words, a text version only if the gate passes, and what not to say. Use the member's Objection Bank answer and proof point when they exist, and mark a generic answer [ADD PROOF]. Never predict rates, prices, or the market, and never guarantee savings from waiting or from acting now. Present both paths honestly as the buyer's choice, point to the lender for the numbers, and end with a low-pressure next step, which can be the nurture plan (job 11).
