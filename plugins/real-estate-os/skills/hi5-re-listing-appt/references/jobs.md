# The Six Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. Follow the loaded module for the client type, and the guardrails file. Every job starts from the member's own details in the profile and asks only for what is missing, in one short batch. Drafts only.

---

## 1. The appointment game plan
Inputs (ask only what is missing; if something important is missing, ask up to 3 questions before writing): the seller's name or names, the property address, what is known about the home (beds, baths, condition, updates, quirks), why they are selling (as they stated it), their ideal timeline, the price they have in mind, what the CMA supports (a range, from job 3 if available), their top priority (top dollar, speed, certainty, a smooth move), how they found the member, competing agents if known, and the member's proof (recent sales, reviews, list to sale ratio, days on market versus the market).

Deliver, under 600 words, in spoken lines the member can say out loud:
1. **The first 3 minutes:** an opener that refers to something specific to them and their home, plus one sentence that sets the agenda.
2. **Discovery:** five questions about their real motivation and decision process (who else decides, what happens if it does not sell, what went wrong with past agents). The most important question goes first. Use the module's questions.
3. **The pricing frame:** four steps for presenting the CMA so they reach the number together, plus one bridge line if their expectation is above the data. Use only the comps the member gave.
4. **Why me:** three differentiators tied to their stated priority, each with one proof point from the member's inputs or profile. If there is no proof, flag where it is needed. Never use a proof point marked internal in anything the seller will see.
5. **Likely objections:** the three they are most likely to raise for their situation, with a two sentence answer for each.
6. **The ask:** a direct, low pressure closing question, and what to say if they say "we need to think about it".
7. **Leave behind checklist:** what to bring or send after.

Then remove anything generic. Offer a one page car cheat sheet (opener, top discovery question, pricing bridge line, three differentiators, closing question, max 150 words).

## 2. The pre-appointment seller intake
Inputs: seller name, property address, appointment date, time, and place, how long it will take, who should attend (everyone on title or involved in the decision), and a link to an intake form if they have one.

Write:
1. A confirmation email under 170 words: confirm the details, explain what you will cover in one sentence, ask everyone involved in the decision to attend, and ask them to answer 6 to 8 short questions by reply or form. It ends with the member's `disclosure_line_full`.
2. The question list, one line each: updates made and roughly when, anything they know needs repair, their ideal move date, what matters most in the sale, what they liked or disliked about past agents, anything about the property a buyer should know, and, for the module's client type, its own questions. Ask about the property, the goals, and the timeline. Do not ask about the household, family, health, or age, and do not ask for legal or financial details that belong with their attorney, CPA, or lender.
3. A short text version under 300 characters, only if texting is allowed (run the consent check; otherwise draft only the email).
4. A day-before reminder text under 200 characters, with the same consent rule.

Tone: organized and warm, no pitch. It must be answerable from a phone in under 5 minutes. Offer to turn the questions into form fields (name, type, required or optional) and a thank-you message for the member's CRM form builder.
To send any of these, use /hi5-re-crm job 11 (one message at a time, after the member approves the exact text). To book the meeting, use job 9.

## 4. The pricing conversation
Inputs: the property, the seller's number, where the comps land, two or three key comps (address, sold price, why it matters), market conditions the member gives, what is driving the seller's number (what they owe, what a neighbor got, an online estimate, money put into upgrades), and their priority. If anything important is missing, ask up to 3 questions.

Deliver, in script style with short bracketed notes, under 450 words:
1. **Before the numbers:** two questions that get them to share where their number comes from, and one line that acknowledges it without agreeing.
2. **Presenting the comps:** a three comp story in spoken language (the best match, one that sold quickly, one that sat), and why buyers compare their home to these.
3. **The gap:** what pricing at their number likely means for showings, days on market, and the eventual price, framed as buyer behavior and not the agent's opinion.
4. **Two options:** a "priced to compete" number and a "test the top" number with a written review date and a pre-agreed adjustment if activity is low. They choose.
5. **If they hold firm:** three answers for "I want to try my price first", "an online estimate says more", and "we put a lot into this house".
6. **Locking it in:** one sentence that confirms the price and the review date and moves to signing.
Nothing pressures, guarantees a sale price, or speaks badly of another agent. Offer to write the "test the top" plan as a short written agreement: list price, review date, the activity benchmarks, and the agreed adjustment, for the member's listing paperwork and their broker to review.

## 5. Objection answers
Inputs: the member's market, proof points, and how they explain their value and what is included. Read the Objection Bank page for this client type and start from the member's own answers. Add the objections for this module (the home seller list is in the module). The member can keep, delete, or add.

For each objection write: **Acknowledge** (one sentence), **Clarify** (one question that finds the real concern), **Respond** (two or three sentences using a specific proof point or market fact), **Advance** (one line that moves forward). Under 5 sentences each, calm and certain, not defensive.
- For any fee objection: compensation is set by agreement between the agent and the client and is negotiable. Never call it standard or fixed. Explain the value and what is included, and use the member's own fee details.
- Never name or criticize another agent or company, and never promise a sale price.
Offer a shorter second version (under 2 sentences) of the three most likely objections, for when the seller cuts the member off. Mark any answer with no specific proof `[ADD PROOF]`.

## 6. Follow up with a seller who has not signed
Inputs: seller name, property, appointment date, what was covered, something personal or specific they said (their move date, a feature they love, a worry, only if they offered it), their main hesitation, the one reason the member is the right fit, and new market information the member can share.

Write 4 touches, each with a subject line (emails only) and one alternate opening line:
1. **Same day or next morning, email, under 130 words:** thank them, recall one specific detail, restate the recommendation in one sentence, give one clear next step.
2. **Day 3, email, under 120 words:** one useful piece of market information tied to their home and their hesitation. A soft call to action.
3. **Day 7, text, under 250 characters:** personal, one easy question. Only with documented consent (see guardrails). If consent is unknown, draft an email instead.
4. **Day 14, email, under 100 words:** a respectful close the loop message that makes it easy to say "not now" and keeps the door open.
Every email ends with the member's `disclosure_line_full` and the required notices. No guilt, no fake urgency, and no "just checking in". Each touch must give them something or ask something specific, and read as if a person wrote it that day.

Offer the two branches after touch 4: "we went with another agent" (gracious, ask for feedback, offer to stay in touch) and "we're waiting" (agree on a check-in date and what the member will send before then).
If the member wants this sequence automated, give instructions for building it as a CRM workflow that stops when the seller replies. Do not build or enroll anything without the member's OK (use /hi5-re-crm job 10). Send each message only through /hi5-re-crm job 11.
