# Module: Land, New Construction, and Commercial Listings

A cautious module. Details differ by property and by state, so the member and their broker or attorney confirm the facts. Never guess.

## Discovery questions
1. What is the property, and how is it zoned or permitted today? (Ask the seller for their documents. Never guess what can be built.)
2. What are your goals: price, timing, or a particular kind of buyer's use?
3. What utilities, access, and survey details do you have, and what is unknown?
4. For new construction: who is the builder, what stage is the project, and what do the builder's contracts and warranties say?
5. For commercial: is it a sale or a lease, is it occupied, and what do the leases say?
6. Who else decides, and who needs to approve a price or terms?

## Pricing notes
- Land: use sales of similar parcels (size, zoning, access, utilities) the member supplies. Do not state what can be built. Due diligence items are for the buyer's and seller's professionals.
- New construction: price against comparable new and resale homes the member supplies, and the builder's current terms.
- Commercial: a broker opinion of value is different from a residential CMA. Use sales or lease comps and any income data (rent roll, expenses) the member supplies. If a cap rate is used, label it the member's assumption and show the math.
- Everything is an opinion of value for the agent, not an appraisal.

## Cautions
- Leases, letters of intent, and purchase agreements for commercial property and land need attorney review.
- Environmental, zoning, survey, and utility questions are for qualified professionals. List them as due diligence items, and do not answer them.
- Treat any advertising for housing as under the Housing special ad category unless the member's broker confirms otherwise. Follow Fair Housing and the brokerage's advertising rules.
- Tax questions, including 1031 exchanges, go to the member's CPA.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "The land is worth more because of what could be built." (Do not state what can be built. Suggest due diligence.)
2. "I want to wait for the market or the area to develop."
3. "Your fee is too high." Compensation is set by agreement and is negotiable. Never call it standard.
4. "I'll talk to buyers directly."
5. The member's Objection Bank answers, if any.

## Follow-up notes
Put attorney and due diligence reviews on the timeline. Every email ends with the disclosure line.
