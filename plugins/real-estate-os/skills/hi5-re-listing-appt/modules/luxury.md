# Module: Luxury Seller

A high-end property where comps are scarce, sales take longer, and privacy matters.

## Discovery questions
1. What matters most: the price, privacy, or the right fit with the buyer's timeline?
2. Is there a date or event that drives your timing?
3. Have you had the property valued or listed before? What happened?
4. Who else needs to approve a price or an offer?
5. What are the signature features, and what have you invested in?
6. How do you want the property shown (by appointment, with notice, with any restrictions)?

## Pricing notes
- Comps are often thin. Widen the radius and the time frame (up to 12 months) only with the reasons stated, and say plainly when a comp is not a close match. Prefer fewer, closer comps to many loose ones.
- Include active and pending properties, and the average days on market for this price range as the member supplies it.
- Value drivers (view, land, craftsmanship, location) described as features of the property. Never describe who the buyers are.

## Marketing and privacy
Ask the seller what may be public (address, photos, price) before any marketing is drafted. Off-market or private showings are the seller's choice. Follow the MLS and brokerage rules on when a property must be marketed.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "I need a number that reflects what I put into it."
2. "An online estimate says my home is worth more."
3. "I want to try a higher price first and wait."
4. "Your fee is too high." Compensation is set by agreement and is negotiable. Never call it standard.
5. "I don't want strangers in my home."
6. The member's Objection Bank answers for luxury sellers.

## Follow-up notes
Be discreet and precise. Offer a clear marketing plan and a review date. Every email ends with the disclosure line.
