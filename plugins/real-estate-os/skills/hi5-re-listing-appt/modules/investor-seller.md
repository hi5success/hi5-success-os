# Module: Investor Selling a Property

An investor selling a rental, a flip, or a portfolio property. The conversation is about numbers, timing, and next steps.

## Discovery questions
1. What is your goal for this sale: the highest price, a fast close, or setting up your next purchase?
2. Is the property occupied by tenants, and what do the leases say (term, rent, renewal)?
3. Do you plan to use the proceeds for another purchase, and do you have a date that matters?
4. What do you owe, and what is the lowest number that works for you?
5. What financials can you share (rent roll, expenses, and recent repairs)?
6. Who else needs to approve the sale (a partner, a lender)?

## Pricing notes
- Use only what the member and investor supply: sales comps, rent roll, income and expenses. If the member wants an income approach, ask for the cap rate they use and label it "your assumption". Show the math.
- Never promise a return, a net amount, or a tax result. Show a gross to net walkthrough only from numbers the investor provides, and label it a rough estimate for them and their CPA.
- A 1031 exchange has strict deadlines. Say it is a question for their CPA and a qualified intermediary, and ask the investor for their timeline so the plan fits it. Do not advise.
- A tenant occupied sale has notice and lease rights that depend on the lease and the state. Flag it for the member's attorney.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "Buyers will price it on income, so I need a higher number."
2. "I need a certain number to complete my exchange."
3. "Your fee is too high." Compensation is set by agreement and is negotiable. Never call it standard.
4. "I'll sell it to another investor myself."
5. "I want to wait for the leases to renew."
6. The member's Objection Bank answers for investors.

## Follow-up notes
Offer a numbers summary the investor can pass to their CPA. Every email ends with the disclosure line.
