# Module: Landlord With a Rental to List

A rental listing appointment. The "price" is the monthly rent, the "comps" are rental comps, and the follow-up is about a leasing plan.

## Discovery questions
1. What do you want this rental to do for you: steady income, the shortest vacancy, or the lowest hassle?
2. Who else decides, and who owns the property (on title)?
3. What is the target move-in date, and what lease length do you want?
4. What has been updated or needs work before showings?
5. Have you rented it before? What worked and what did not?
6. Do you want help managing it after it is leased, or only help finding a tenant?

## Pricing notes
- Use rental comps the member supplies: asking and leased rents, days to lease, size, beds and baths, and amenities. Same method as the CMA: show each adjustment, label it an estimate. Rent is the agent's recommendation, not a guarantee.
- Include the move-in cost sheet the member's market uses (rent, deposit, fees) only from facts the member gives. Security deposit, fee, and notice rules differ by state and city, so tell the member to confirm them with their broker or attorney. Never state a legal limit.

## Leasing plan (outline)
Showing plan, listing description (features of the property only), application process, written screening criteria, lease start, and a renewal date. The lease itself is a legal document: say it needs attorney review.

## Fair Housing for rentals (always)
- Screening criteria must be written down and applied the same way to every applicant.
- If a landlord says they want a certain kind of tenant (by family, age, religion, origin, disability, or anything protected), do not draft around it. Say it is a Fair Housing risk, and offer written, neutral criteria (for example income to rent ratio and rental history) applied to everyone.
- Service and assistance animals are not pets. Ask the member's attorney about the rules in their state.
- State and local laws can protect more classes, such as source of income. Read `protected_class_jurisdictions`.

## Objections (keep, delete, or add)
1. "I'll just manage it myself."
2. "Your fee is too high." Compensation is set by agreement and is negotiable. Never call it standard.
3. "I want to ask for more rent than the comps show."
4. "I only want to rent to a certain kind of tenant." (Use the Fair Housing response above.)
5. "I don't want to make repairs before showings."
6. The member's Objection Bank answers for landlords.

## Follow-up notes
Offer a leasing timeline and a day 14 close the loop. Every email ends with the disclosure line.
