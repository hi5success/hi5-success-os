# The Nine Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. Job 1 is in `expired-research.md`. For every job: scan first (the CRM, the profile, the CRM Map, earlier Marketing Hub rows, and the list or facts the member gave), ask only about what could not be found, and point to what you found. Every touch passes the gate in `../../hi5-re-sphere/references/touch-rules.md` and shows the result. Honest identity on every piece. Never state a value. Drafts only. Mail and email end with the disclosure line. If something important is still missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

## Expired and FSBO owners

### 2. The expired owner comeback sequence
Inputs to look for: the address, the original price and changes, days on market, the diagnosis of why it did not sell (from job 1 or the member), the one concrete thing the member would do differently, proof the member can use (a similar home they sold, their own average days on market, only if `proof_point_public` allows it), and which channels pass the gate.
Open with the gate result as a short reminder at the top. Write a 5-touch sequence, each in a channel that passes:
1. **Day 1, a letter** (under 170 words): acknowledge the frustration without blame, name the specific fix, and offer a short conversation. Never claim the listing failed because of the owner or the previous agent.
2. **Day 3, an email** (under 100 words), if the member has an email for the owner from the owner or their own records.
3. **Day 5, a call script and a 20 second voicemail**, only if the member has confirmed the scrub (see `lists-and-farms.md`).
4. **Day 8, a text**, only if the member has documented texting consent for that person.
5. **Day 14, a handwritten note or a second letter** with a useful fact the member provides (never a made-up number).
Every touch states who the member is and which brokerage, and offers a free consultation or a CMA. When the gate blocks a channel, write the next touch in a channel that passes and say what changed. Offer an A/B version of the Day 1 letter (leading with the pricing story or the marketing fix) and what to track.
If the owner says stop, follow the opt-out rule. If the owner relists with someone else, stop.

### 3. The FSBO help sequence
Inputs to look for: the address, the asking price, how long it has been listed, where it is advertised, one helpful thing the member can offer that is hard to do alone (a buyer screening checklist, a contract checklist, showing safety tips, the member's own marketing outline), and which channels pass the gate.
Write a 4-touch sequence in channels that pass the gate, each under 120 words: a helpful letter or note that offers the resource with nothing required in return, a follow-up that delivers it, a short call script (only if the scrub is confirmed) that asks how it is going and offers to answer questions, and a last note that leaves the door open. Never criticize the owner's choice to sell on their own, never suggest the owner is doing something wrong, never imply a relationship, and never promise a result. Never contact an owner who has put a "no agents" or "no solicitations" notice in their listing. Offer to build the one-page resource the member promised as a branded checklist.

## Your farm

### 4. Set up or review a farm
Follow `lists-and-farms.md` (choosing and reviewing a farm). Output the farm criteria the member used (business facts only), the 12-month plan table, and the measures the member will track. Refuse any criteria based on who lives there, and say which rule.

### 5. This month's farm letter
Inputs to look for: the farm and month, recent sales the member can reference (only the member's own sales, or where the MLS and brokerage rules allow it, and `sold_price_policy`), one market number the member provides with its source and date, a local note (optional), and the response offer (a home value update, a neighborhood sales report, or a text-for-info line). A text-for-info line needs the member's documented consent flow, so say once to check it with their broker, and do not offer one if the member has no consent process.
Write 3 versions, each under 180 words, with a headline and a P.S.: a just-sold story (only where allowed), a market update in 3 plain sentences using only the member's number, and a local note with a seasonal tip. Each states who the member is, carries the disclosure line and required notices, and follows `brokerage_ad_rules`. No value statements, no steering phrases, no claims about the people nearby. Offer the 12-month letter rotation from the farm plan.

### 6. A door-knock script
Start with the reminder to check local solicitation rules and any HOA rules, skip homes posted "No Soliciting", and log any "do not come back" as an internal do-not-contact request.
Inputs to look for: the farm, the reason for knocking (just sold nearby, a market update, an introduction, or an event invitation), the detail to reference (only a fact the member provides), and the leave-behind.
Write a script under 90 seconds: an opener that says who the member is and why they are on this street, one value line, an open question (for example "What have you heard about what's selling on this street?"), two follow-up questions if they mention selling (timeline and next step) ending with an offer of a home value update, and a graceful exit if they are not interested. Add a "not home" leave-behind note (under 40 words) and a short log template (address, result, follow-up) the member can use to record visits. Door knocks collect no phone number unless the person offers it and says they are fine being contacted, and if they give a number, the member notes how and when they gave it.

## Conversations

### 7. Call and voicemail scripts
Write only after the member confirms the scrub for the number in this conversation, and only inside 8am to 9pm in the person's time zone. If not confirmed, say so and offer a letter or email instead.
Write: a 30 second opener that says the member's name, brokerage, and why they are calling, asks permission to continue, and offers one helpful thing, with a clean exit if they say no; a 20 second voicemail that does not mention a price, a value, or the owner's circumstances; and a one line way to honor "do not call me again". No automated, prerecorded, or artificial voice. If they say stop, thank them, end the call, and plan to log them as do-not-contact after the member's OK.

### 8. Equity conversation questions
For homeowners in the farm or the member's own list who have not raised selling. Write 6 open questions that explore their plans and their home (what they love about it, whether they have thought about a change in the next few years, what would make a move worth it, whether they have projects planned) and a closing offer of a free home value update. Never mention a value, equity, or appreciation as fact, never ask about family, age, health, or income, and never suggest they should move. Add a 2 sentence way to book the update.

### 9. Handle a reply or an objection
For a reply to a letter, email, text, or call. Paste or describe what they said. Write 2 short replies (under 70 words) in the channel they used (a text reply only if the gate passes), that answer what they said, take the pressure off, and offer one next step. Cover these cases when they come up: "I'm already working with an agent" (thank them and leave the door open, and never ask them to break an agreement), "I want to try on my own" (offer the helpful resource), "what's my home worth" (offer a CMA, never a number), and "stop contacting me" (confirm, apologize briefly, and plan the opt-out with the member's OK). Add a 3 line coaching note on the tone.
