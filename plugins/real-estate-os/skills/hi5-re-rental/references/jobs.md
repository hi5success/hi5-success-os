# The Seven Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (the CRM record, the profile, earlier Marketing Hub rows for this property, the lease or documents the member gave), ask only about what could not be found, and point to what you found. Figures come only from the owner and from comps the member supplies. Leases and notices are for the owner's attorney. Drafts only. Emails end with the disclosure line. If something important is missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

---

## Price and list

### 1. The rent pricing brief
Inputs to look for: the property (size, beds and baths, features, condition and updates), the owner's goal (steady income, shortest vacancy, or lowest effort), the target move-in date, the lease length, and the rental comps the member supplies (asking and leased rents, days to lease, size, beds and baths, amenities, and the date of each).
Build:
1. **A comps table** exactly as supplied: address, rent, size, beds and baths, days to lease, and date, marked asking or leased. Say when a comp is a stretch, and when there are fewer than 3 close comps.
2. **Adjustments shown line by line** (size, condition, amenities, parking), each labeled as the member's estimate. Never invent an adjustment amount. Ask the member for the numbers they use.
3. **A recommended range** for the member to present, labeled the agent's recommendation and not a guarantee, with what would move it up or down (the condition, the move-in date, concessions the owner is willing to give).
4. **The move-in cost sheet** from the owner's figures only (first month, deposit, fees, pet terms) with "confirm with the owner and your attorney" on anything that depends on a state or city rule. Never state a legal limit.
5. **Three talking points** for the owner on pricing against vacancy.
If the member supplied no comps, do not suggest a rent. Say which comps are needed and offer to build the request, or point to /hi5-re-listing-appt for the comps method.

### 2. Written screening criteria
Inputs to look for: what the owner decides to require. Start from what the owner says, and fill nothing in for them.
Build a one-page sheet, "Our written rental criteria", with each item stated as a measurable condition the owner chose: a complete application, a rent-to-income ratio or income requirement if the owner uses one, how rental history and landlord references are checked, how credit and background results are used and what the owner will and will not count, pet and assistance animal handling (assistance animals are handled as the owner's attorney advises), the occupancy standard the owner states (taking Fair Housing into account, which the attorney confirms), and the application fee and deposit as the owner sets them. Add: "These criteria apply to every applicant in the same way."
Flag and decline any criterion that screens by a protected class or a proxy. For source of income, say many places protect it, read `protected_class_jurisdictions`, and send the question to the attorney. End with: "Have your attorney review these criteria before you use them." Give the owner a short explanation they can hand to an applicant who asks how decisions are made.

### 3. The rental listing plan
Inputs to look for: the property facts, the rent the owner chose, the move-in date, the lease term, the showing approach, and the application steps.
Build: a facts-only listing description draft (rent, term, available date, what is included, beds and baths, features, pet terms as the owner states them, and the application steps), the showing plan (dates, how access works, a safety note for the member), the application process in order, a reply to an inquiry (under 80 words, email, or a text only if the gate passes), and the checklist to launch. Never state a tenant preference. Point to /hi5-re-listing-launch for the full launch kit and /hi5-re-ads for ads.

## Lease-up

### 4. The applications tracker and decision script
Inputs to look for: the written criteria (job 2) and the applications the owner or member has received.
Build:
1. **A tracker** with Applicant 1, 2, 3 (no names), the date received, each criterion marked met, not met, or documents missing (as the documents show), and the next step. No household details, no health, no notes about who someone is.
2. **A decision script.** For each decision, one paragraph that cites only the written criteria and the documents, for the owner to use. If more than one applicant meets the criteria, say the owner follows the order of completed applications or the tie-break the owner wrote in the criteria, and that the attorney confirms it.
3. **A message to an applicant** who was approved or not approved. For a not-approved applicant, use the placeholder [ADVERSE ACTION NOTICE: confirm the required wording with your attorney] and a short, kind covering note. Never state what the notice must say.
4. **The signing steps:** the lease review with the owner's attorney, the deposit and first month as stated in the lease, the move-in condition report, and the keys. Point to /hi5-re-transaction for the landlord lease checklist and key dates.

## Keep it running

### 5. The renewal plan
Inputs to look for: the lease end date and any renewal or notice dates, read from the lease only. If the lease is not in hand, ask for it, and put "confirm from the lease" in every date column until it is.
Build: a timeline working back from the lease end (when to talk to the tenant, when to decide on a rent change, and when to send a renewal offer or a non-renewal notice), each date taken from the lease and the owner's attorney, never from a general rule. Include a renewal conversation script (open, ask about their experience, share the owner's decision), a rent change recommendation only if the member supplies updated comps (and the owner's decision), a renewal offer letter outline for the owner and attorney to complete, and a non-renewal checklist for the owner and attorney. Never state notice periods or limits on rent changes.

### 6. The move-out checklist
Build: the owner's move-out instructions to the tenant (cleaning, keys, forwarding address, final utilities), an inspection checklist by room that compares to the move-in condition report with photos and dates, a deposit accounting worksheet that lists the deposit, each deduction with the invoice or the receipt, and the balance, all from the owner's records, and the steps to make the unit ready to list. Never state deadlines, allowed deductions, or notice requirements. Add: "Your attorney confirms how and when the deposit accounting must be sent."

### 7. The monthly owner update
Inputs to look for: showings, applications, leasing status, maintenance items, and what is next. Write an email under 150 words in the member's voice: a one-line status, what happened this month, what is next, anything the owner needs to decide, and one calm closing sentence. If it was a slow month, say so directly and say what the member is doing about it. Never blame the owner, the market, or applicants, and never state a market figure the member did not give.
