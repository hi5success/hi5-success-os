# The Twelve Jobs

Used by `SKILL.md`. For every job: scan first (the CRM record or list, the profile, the CRM Map, earlier Marketing Hub rows, and anything the member pasted), ask only about what could not be found, and point to what you found. Every touch passes the gate in `touch-rules.md` and shows the result. Use only details the client shared. Drafts only. Every email ends with the disclosure line. If something important is still missing after the scan, ask up to 3 questions before writing.

Never use the words "checking in" or "just checking" in a subject line or a message body (a subject like "Thinking of you" or a specific detail works better). Messages avoid "just checking in", never pitch in a first touch, and never contain a market number or a value the member did not give.

---

## Plan the year

### 1. The database touch plan
Inputs to look for: the contacts in the CRM (or the pasted list) with relationship type, closing date, last touch, and tags, how many touches a week the member can keep up, and the channels they use.
1. Sort the database into segments on business facts only: past clients by closing year, sphere (friends, family, vendors, neighbors), active nurture leads, and cold contacts (12 months or more with no touch). Never segment by a protected characteristic or a neighborhood's makeup.
2. Show the segment table (Segment, Count, What they get, How often, Channel) and a realistic monthly rhythm with a weekly cap, for example two personal touches and one value touch per week, using only the member's stated capacity.
3. Name who is due now: the 10 people with the oldest last touch and the strongest relationship, and the one touch for each.
4. List contacts with too little information and give 2 questions to ask each next time they talk.
5. List contacts the gate skips (do-not-disturb, unsubscribed, no consent for texts) and what they get instead.
6. Offer to create the CRM tasks with /hi5-re-crm (job 5) after the member's OK.

### 2. A 12-month post-close plan for one client
Inputs to look for: the client's name, which side the member represented, the property, the closing date, the review link, the member's referral approach, and what the CRM notes say they care about. Read the transaction record and the notes first.
Build a table: When (day 3, week 2, month 1, 3, 6, the closing anniversary, and month 11), Channel, Purpose, and the message written out (under 80 words each). Include:
- A move-in check (for a landlord or tenant file, a first-month check).
- The review ask at the right moment (see job 10).
- Homeowner value items such as seasonal maintenance reminders and a tax-related reminder worded "check with your county or CPA". Never state a state's rule or an amount.
- One referral ask framed as help (see job 9), never earlier than month 3.
- A home value check at the anniversary, offered as a free update the member runs, never a number.
Mark which touches are automatic drafts and which create a task for the member (a call, a card, a pop-by). Use only the first name or a placeholder like [CLIENT FIRST NAME] when the name is not in the record. Texts only where the gate passes, and say what replaces them when it does not. Never write a touch that conditions a gift on a referral or a review.
Hand-off: show the full list of CRM tasks and calendar events as a plan, with the dates counted from the closing date in the record, and create them only after the member's OK, through /hi5-re-crm (job 5) and the calendar connection. If a closing date is not in the record, show "confirm closing date" in the When column and do not compute dates.

### 3. The milestone calendar
Inputs to look for: past clients with closing dates and the milestones they shared (a pet, a kids' school milestone they mentioned, a business event, a hobby), found in the CRM notes. Birthdays only if the member's CRM already has the field and the client gave it, and only to prompt a card.
Build a 12-month table: Date, Client, Trigger, What to send or do, Channel, Short message (under 30 words), and Cost. Rules: no more than one touch per client per month, no week with more than the member's weekly cap, and 3 to 4 big moments per client per year (the home anniversary plus their strongest personal milestone). Fill gaps with a photo or a local tip, not a generic holiday. Flag clients with too little information and give 2 questions to ask each. Use only details they shared or posted publicly. Offer to create the calendar events after the member's OK.

## Reach out

### 4. Past-client reconnect (a batch of Gmail drafts)
Inputs to look for: how many years back (1, 2, or 3 by default), how many days with no touch (90 by default), and the batch size (10 by default). Say the defaults you will use and let the member change them.
1. Find past clients whose closing was that many years ago and who have no logged contact in that window, closest anniversary first. Read the CRM only.
2. For each, show the name, property, side, closing date, and the notes you will use (only what is in the notes).
3. Show the batch plan (Person, Channel, Gate result, Draft to be saved). Draft each as an email under 100 words: open with something specific to them or their home (never a market statistic), give one piece of value (a seasonal home-maintenance reminder, or a neighborhood note the member gave you), and end with a low-pressure question. No "just checking in", no referral ask in the first reconnect, no market numbers.
4. Save the drafts to Gmail only after the member's OK for the batch. Never send. Tell the member how many drafts were saved and which people were skipped and why.
5. Offer to log the touches with /hi5-re-crm (job 4) after they send.

### 5. No-agenda check-in texts
Inputs to look for: who it is for (one person or a list from the CRM), the relationship, and what the member last talked about with them.
1. Run the gate for each person first. Show the result table. Anyone who fails goes to a short email version instead, or is skipped.
2. For each person who passes, write 2 text options under 30 words that sound like a friend and give them an easy reply. Include nothing about real estate unless the person raised it, and never a link in the first text.
3. If the member sends through a platform, remind them of the A2P registration and to include whatever opt-out wording their platform requires.
4. Never write a list-wide text as a single blast, and never send. Each message is a draft for the member to send one at a time with /hi5-re-crm job 11 after approving the exact text.
Offer the reply handler (job 8) for when they answer.

### 6. The home anniversary note and equity conversation
Inputs to look for: the client, the closing date, the property, and what the notes say about the home (a project they planned, a feature they loved).
Write one email under 100 words and one text version (only if the gate passes): celebrate the home, mention one real detail from the notes, and offer a free home value update "if you're curious what it's worth today". Add one 3-question talk track for the member's call that explores plans (what they are thinking about the home, their timeline, what would have to be true to move) and never asks about family, age, or health. Never state a value, equity, or appreciation unless the member gives the figure and its source. If the member wants a value, point to /hi5-re-listing-appt for a CMA.

### 7. A timely note after a life event or a market change
Inputs to look for: the event (a job change, a nearby sale, a rate change, a season, a local event) and the one person it connects to. The event must be something the person shared, something public, or a fact the member gives you. Write one short note under 80 words that connects the event to that one person and offers one small helpful thing. Never mention a protected characteristic, never guess at an event, and never say a rate or price number the member did not give. Run the gate for the channel.

### 8. Wake up cold clients (3 emails and a reply handler)
For past clients or leads with 12 months or more of silence.
1. A 3-email sequence, one week apart, under 90 words each: a warm, specific hello, one useful thing from the member's own knowledge, and a gentle door-open. No guilt, no "we haven't heard from you", no pressure. Each email has the disclosure line and the member's unsubscribe line. Skip anyone who is do-not-disturb or unsubscribed.
2. A reply handler for when they respond: 3 short replies, one for a warm reply, one for a question about the market (point to a CMA or a call, never a figure), and one for "not now" (thank them and leave the door open, no pushing). If they say stop, follow the opt-out rule in `touch-rules.md`.
Offer to build the sequence as a GoHighLevel workflow outline (trigger, waits, messages, which steps create a task for the member), then run it by /hi5-re-crm job 10 after the member's OK.

## Ask and thank

### 9. The referral ask
Inputs to look for: the client, what the client said or did that shows they were happy (a note, a text, a review they left), and how long since closing.
Write one message under 80 words that thanks them for something specific, asks plainly "If someone you know is thinking about a move, I'd be glad to help", and gives one easy next step (reply with a name, or pass along a contact card). Also write a short version for the member to say on a call. It never offers anything of value for the referral, never creates urgency, and never asks the client to share another person's private information. Tell the member to follow their brokerage's referral rules. When a referral arrives, point to /hi5-re-crm (job 3) to log it, and never invent the referrer's name.

### 10. The review ask
Inputs to look for: the client, the review platform and link (ask for the link each time, and never save it to the profile), and one real moment from the transaction in the notes.
Write one email and one text version (text only if the gate passes), each under 70 words, that recalls the specific moment, makes the request plain, and includes the link. It never offers anything of value for a review, never asks for a positive review, and never asks only some clients. The member follows the review site's rules and their brokerage's rules for testimonials. Add the disclosure line to the email.

### 11. Closing gift ideas and card notes, and pop-bys
Inputs to look for: the client, why they moved, a moment or detail from the notes, the home, a budget, and the timing (closing day, first week, or a pop-by later).
Give 5 ideas across three types: a story gift that captures a moment from the deal, a life gift that fits how they will use the home, and a first-week gift that solves a real move-in problem. For each: the detail it ties to, where to get or make it, the best delivery day and why, the cost, and a card message under 50 words. For a pop-by, give the idea, the drop-off script (leave it at the door, under 20 seconds, no pitch), and a tip to check any HOA or solicitation rule. No gift is tied to a referral or a review, no card says "refer me", and the member checks their brokerage's gift rules. This skill only suggests and writes. It never orders or spends anything.

### 12. Handwritten notes for the week
Pull 5 people from the database touch plan or the member's list who are due a personal touch and have a real detail in the CRM. For each, write a note of 25 to 40 words that references that detail and ends warmly, with no pitch and no ask. Leave a placeholder where the detail is missing and say so. Offer to log the five touches with /hi5-re-crm (job 4) after the member sends them.
