# Styles

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 2. Pick the style before the type template. Defaults only: the member's approved emails beat everything here.

## The styles
1. **Plain friend note (the default).** 50 to 180 words. One link, no images, little or no bold, short paragraphs of one or two lines, signed by a person, an optional P.S. It reads like a message from someone you know. Best for tips, nurture, check-ins, re-engagement, asks, and for solo businesses, creators, and agents. Most good emails in a real inbox look like this.
2. **Branded value column.** The same name every time, 270 to 450 words. A fixed shape: one quote or hook line, a title, the problem, the fix, a short example or math, then an actions list. Best for building authority, for B2B, and for a regular market or industry minute.
3. **Long-form story.** 450 to 800 words (a founder letter may run longer, once per launch). Opens in the middle of a scene with a time or place, then the tension, what was done, the lesson, and the offer as the next step. Use at most once a week. Best for launches, a founder voice, and warming a cold list.
4. **Tutorial.** 900 words or more. Numbered steps, copy and paste blocks, one deliverable. Best for software and education brands that promise free value.
5. **Designed digest.** Logo and fixed labeled sections, 450 to 800 words, a handful to a couple dozen links. Best for monthly client newsletters, brokerages, and enterprise brands.
6. **Warm launch sequence.** A run of plain notes and one story that moves from useful to direct: open, value, proof, objections, what is inside, last call. One a day, rising only in the last two to three days of a real deadline. A personal sign-off and bracket status tags such as [last call]. Best for programs, memberships, and open-house or listing weeks.
7. **Event reminder ladder.** Confirm, 24 hours, day of, 1 hour or 10 minutes, replay, replay deadline. Each 60 to 150 words, one link, and the same P.S. with the date, time, and time zone. Best for webinars, open houses, seminars.
8. **Lifecycle automation.** Triggered by what the reader did: a purchase, a milestone, an abandoned checkout, a failed payment, inactivity. Short and specific to the reader's own data. Best for software, memberships, and CRM-driven follow-up.
9. **Member notice.** The topic, why it matters, the time with the time zone, and one join link. Best for memberships and team or brokerage announcements.

## Do not use: the hard launch blitz
All caps warnings, "final notice" subjects, a name in every subject, five or six sends in one day, and unproven income claims. It trains readers to ignore or report the sender, it breaks the honest-urgency rule, and the claims are unsafe. Borrow only this: the last day of a real deadline can carry two or three short emails.

## Which style to start with
| Email type | Default style | Switch when |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | Plain friend note | Enterprise account setup: lifecycle |
| Nurture or value | Plain friend note | Authority building or B2B: branded value column |
| Story | Long-form story | Keep to 450 to 800 words for most lists |
| Sales or launch | Warm launch sequence | One flash offer: plain friend note |
| Flash offer | Plain friend note | None |
| Book a call | Plain friend note | B2B with a case study: branded value column |
| Event invite and reminders | Event reminder ladder | A single reminder: plain friend note |
| Post-purchase | Lifecycle automation | Warm creator or agent: plain friend note |
| Abandoned checkout | Lifecycle automation | None |
| Win-back | Plain friend note | A code sequence over three days |
| Review or referral ask | Plain friend note | None |
| Check-in or research | Plain friend note | None |
| Milestone | Lifecycle automation | None |
| Update or notice | Designed digest or member notice | A small change: plain friend note |
| Broadcast announcement | Plain friend note | Several items: designed digest |
| Newsletter issue | Designed digest | A personal weekly note: plain friend note |
