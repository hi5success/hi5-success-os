# Sales and Launch Email

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 3. Everything below is a default: the member's approved emails and profile win.

## When to use
Opening and closing an offer to a list: a program, a membership, a product, or a service. Used for each email in a warm launch.

## Default style
Warm launch sequence made of plain friend notes (120 to 250 words) and one story email. This band replaces the general plain note band for launch emails. The last call sends are shorter (the morning one under 120 words, the final one under 80).

## Rules for this type
- One job per email: open, value, proof, objections, what is inside, last call. Do not mix them.
- Every P.S. carries the real deadline with the time zone. Escalate to two or three sends only in the last 2 to 3 days.
- Use bracket status tags on deadline emails: [last call], [closing tonight].
- The next-tier line appears in the P.S. of sales emails and in the body of the "no time" objection, never as the main call to action.
- Price comes after the cost of waiting. Every price, bonus, link, and deadline is a placeholder until the member supplies it.
- Follow the selling moves and guardrails exactly, including refund wording.

## Template
Open: "You are hearing this first." Subject: [tag or number] + the change · Body: why it was built, what it is in one line, who it is for, early bonus and its end date, one link. P.S.: the deadline.
Value: one quick win, no pitch. Subject: a specific number. P.S.: the offer link and deadline.
Proof: one named, permitted result with one number. Subject: the result. P.S.: the deadline.
Objections: "The 3 things holding people back": quote each, answer in one or two lines. Include the next-tier line under the no-time objection.
What is inside: three to five pieces grouped by result, the price, the expiring bonus, one link.
Last call: morning, afternoon, and about three hours before close with [last call] in the subject. Every P.S.: the exact close time with the time zone.
Placeholders: [PRICE], [DEADLINE: day, date, time, time zone], [LINK: checkout], [PROOF NEEDED], [GUARANTEE: paste terms]

## Examples (fictional, model the shape, not the words)
Example (a neutral online course, objections email):
> Subject: 3 reasons people wait
> "I do not have time." The course is 20 minutes a day, and each lesson ends with one thing to finish. If even that is too much, [NEXT TIER: done-for-you option and link].
> "I tried this before and it did not stick." That is the old way: [OLD METHOD]. This version is built around [DIFFERENT APPROACH].
> "Is it worth the price?" Staying where you are costs [COST OF WAITING, supplied by the member]. The course is [PRICE].
> Join here: [LINK: checkout]
> P.S. Closes [DEADLINE: day, date, time, time zone].
Notes: three objections quoted, short honest answers, the next-tier line sits under the no-time objection, and the cost of waiting comes before the price.

## Real estate angle
Use for a new listing week, an open house series, or a home buyer workshop. Proof is a recent sale with real numbers and permission. Never an income claim.

## Business to business or creator angle
A product launch with a demo or webinar and no countdown hype, or a free class, cart open for 5 to 7 days, closing with a story and the guarantee exactly as written.

## Common mistakes
- Fake scarcity or a "last ever" that comes back.
- Stacking several offers in one email.
- A guarantee line that differs from the terms.
- Going to three sends a day before the last 2 to 3 days.
