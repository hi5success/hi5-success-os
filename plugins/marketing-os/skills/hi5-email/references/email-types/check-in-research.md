# Check-In and Research Email

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 3. Everything below is a default: the member's approved emails and profile win.

## When to use
A one-to-one note to understand why someone is stuck or has not acted, or to learn what a list needs.

## Default style
Plain friend note, 60 to 140 words, written like a person.

## Rules for this type
- Say who you are and why you are writing. Name a specific, true behavior only if the member has that data.
- State that this is to understand, not to pitch.
- Offer a short call and also a two-line reply option so it is easy to answer.
- Tell them what the answer will change. Do not promise anything else.

## Template
Subject pattern: "A quick question about your [plan or home search]"
Body beats:
1. "I am [Name], [role]."
2. "I noticed [SPECIFIC BEHAVIOR, only if known]. I would like to understand why, not to pitch you."
3. "Could I grab 15 minutes? [LINK: calendar]"
4. "If a call does not work, a two-line reply covers it:" two questions.
5. "This directly shapes what we do next."

## Examples (fictional, model the shape, not the words)
Example (a neutral fitness studio):
> Subject: a quick question about your membership
> I am [Name], I run the studio. I noticed you have not booked a class in a while, and I would like to understand why, not to pitch you.
> If you have 15 minutes: [LINK: calendar]. Or two lines back is plenty: What got in the way? What would make a class easier to fit in?
> Your answers shape our schedule.
Notes: a true behavior (placeholder if the member does not have it), no pitch, two easy reply questions.

## Real estate angle
"Quick question: is spring or summer better for your move?" One easy question.

## Business to business or creator angle
A named person asks why a feature is not used. Creators: "what is stopping you?"

## Common mistakes
- Pretending to know what you do not.
- A hidden pitch.
- A long list of questions.
- No easy way to reply.
