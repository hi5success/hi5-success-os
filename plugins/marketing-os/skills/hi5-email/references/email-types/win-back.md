# Win-Back Email

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 3. Everything below is a default: the member's approved emails and profile win.

## When to use
People who bought before or went quiet: past buyers, past trial users, inactive subscribers.

## Default style
Plain friend note, 80 to 200 words. A code offer runs as three emails over three days.

## Rules for this type
- A reason, the math, and a deadline. If something broke, say so honestly in an apology email.
- The offer must be real, with its true end date and the exact terms.
- For past buyers, thank them for the purchase by name of the product. For trial users, name the friction and what changed, only if the member supplied it.
- Never shame the reader for leaving.

## Template
Past buyer with a code (3 emails):
1. "You have done this before, so here is your code": thanks for [PAST PRODUCT], the offer, the code, [DEADLINE: day, date, time, time zone].
2. "You have a credit": the math (the normal price against theirs, and what is extra), the deadline.
3. Last call: restate the code, price, and deadline. If anything broke, "the code works now, sorry about that".
Free trial or inactive reader (1 email): "When you tried [PRODUCT] before, [FRICTION]. Now [WHAT CHANGED]." then a risk-free offer and a deadline, or a one-question reply.
P.S.: the code and the deadline

## Examples (fictional, model the shape, not the words)
Example (a neutral meal-planning app, email 2):
> Subject: your [AMOUNT] credit
> Preview: the math is below
> The plan is normally [PRICE]. With your code it is [YOUR PRICE], and the family add-on is [EXTRA].
> Use it here: [LINK: offer], ends [DEADLINE: day, date, time, time zone].
> P.S. Code: [CODE].
Notes: the math is short and uses supplied numbers, the deadline is exact.

## Real estate angle
"Are you still thinking about moving?" A one-question email the reader can answer with yes or no.

## Business to business or creator angle
What changed plus an incentive. Creators: a past-buyer code, the credit math, then the last call.

## Common mistakes
- A fake deadline.
- Guilt.
- Hiding a real mistake.
- A code with no end date.
