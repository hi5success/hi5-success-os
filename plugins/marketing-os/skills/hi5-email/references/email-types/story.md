# Story Email

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 3. Everything below is a default: the member's approved emails and profile win.

## When to use
A founder or personal story that builds trust before an offer, warms a cold list, or explains why the member built something. Use at most once a week.

## Default style
Long-form story, 450 to 800 words (a founder letter up to about 1,500, once per launch). A shorter story can be a plain friend note.

## Rules for this type
- Open in the middle of a scene with a time or a place, not with background.
- Real details only: use what the member gave you. Never invent a scene, a quote, a number, or a customer.
- One lesson, one next step. The offer comes last, as the next step, not the point.
- Cut it to 450 to 800 words unless the member asks for more.
- The subject can be a few words, a time, or a number, and lowercase suits a personal brand.

## Template
Subject patterns: the moment in 1 to 3 words · a time or a number · "i almost [thing] on [day]"
Preview: a time or place detail, or "But it taught me..."
Body beats:
1. The scene with a time or a place: [scene, from the member].
2. The tension and what happened.
3. The turn: what changed.
4. The lesson in one sentence.
5. One next step: [LINK: name] or a three item challenge.
P.S.: a reply keyword the member confirms they will answer, or a one line recap of the offer

## Examples (fictional, model the shape, not the words)
Example (a neutral coaching business, placeholders where facts are missing):
> Subject: 6:10 am
> At 6:10 on a Tuesday I was in the parking lot, deciding whether to go in. [SCENE DETAIL: what the member remembers]
> I almost left. I went in. [WHAT HAPPENED]
> That day taught me one thing: the first five minutes decide the rest.
> If you want help with your first five minutes, here is where I start: [LINK: resource].
> [Name]
Notes: the member supplies the real scene, the lesson is one sentence, and the offer is the next step.

## Real estate angle
A client move story (with their permission) or "why I got into real estate", with a single link to book or reply.

## Business to business or creator angle
A customer story with one number the member can back up, or the "what went wrong" story behind a product.

## Common mistakes
- Inventing details to make it vivid.
- Starting with background.
- More than one lesson.
- Sending a story every day.
