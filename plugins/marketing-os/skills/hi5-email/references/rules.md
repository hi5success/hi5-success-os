# Craft Rules

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 3, read once per run. These apply to every email. A type file adds rules for its own type.

## One job
- One email, one job, one primary call to action. The same link appears at most 3 times (once in the body, once at the end, once in the P.S.).
- Put the key point in the first 2 lines. Inbox summaries read the top first, so a reader who sees only those lines should still get the point.

## Subject and preview
- Subjects run 1 to 9 words. Rotate the pattern: a specific number, a confession, a contrarian claim, a status tag in brackets ([tomorrow], [replay], [last call], [Action Required], [No Action Needed]), a one-word curiosity, or the reader's own data. Lowercase is fine for a personal brand, and Title Case suits a company. Put the reader's name in a subject rarely.
- Preview text is a second hook that adds something the subject did not say. Never repeat the subject. Aim for about 40 to 90 characters.
- At most one emoji in a subject, and none if the brand is formal.

## Opening and close
- Open with a stat, a scene with a time or place, a question the reader is already asking, or a confession. Never open with a greeting about how they are doing or hoping they are well.
- Sign as a person, except for system emails from a company.
- Use the P.S. for the deadline, the event repeat, a second resource, or a next-tier exit ramp (only where the type file and selling file allow it).
- A reply or support line ("hit reply and I will help", "I read every reply") is a commitment. Include it only if the member confirmed someone will do it, otherwise write [confirm: who answers replies].
- Ask for a reply at least once a month, using [confirm: who answers replies] until the member confirms who does. A one-word reply prompt works, but only promise what the member has confirmed they will send.
- Personalize with the reader's own behavior or data when it is available and permitted (what they bought, what they finished, a number from their account), not just their name. Never use data the member does not have.

## Length bands (words)
Plain friend note 50 to 180 · reminder 60 to 150 · pitch 120 to 220 · post-purchase 80 to 200 · branded column 270 to 450 · story 450 to 800 (a founder letter up to about 1,500, once per launch) · digest 450 to 800 · tutorial 900 or more. Launch emails use the band in the sales and launch type file instead. When in doubt, shorter wins. A "we are live" email stays under 150 words.

## Formatting for plain notes
Paragraphs of one or two lines. Bullets only for three or more items. No images, little or no bold, one text link rather than a button.

## Frequency and timing
- At most one email a day, except in the last 2 to 3 days of a real deadline, when up to three short sends in a day are fine (never more than three).
- Send in the audience's time zone, in the morning (7 to 11 AM their time) for value emails. Triggered emails (access, receipts, reminders, abandoned checkout) go out when the trigger fires. A launch may add a noon and an evening send on the last day only. Never schedule by the sender's time zone if it differs.
- Keep a fixed send slot for any recurring email, and name any recurring section the same way each time.

## Never
- A fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" in a subject, fake urgency or scarcity, a deadline that is not real, or ALL CAPS warnings.
- Several access or welcome emails stacked at once.
- Hidden filler text in the preview area or footer, or 10 or more links in a plain email.
- Shaming the reader, slang that does not fit the brand, or a guilt line for not buying.
- Income or earnings claims, unless the member supplied them, marked them public, and they appear with a disclaimer. Never for real estate or agent audiences.
- An event email without the date, time, and time zone in the body.

## The AI-tell list (find and rewrite)
- Negative parallelisms: "it is not X, it is Y".
- Three-beat staccato lines and padding in threes.
- Hype and buzzwords: supercharge, unlock, game-changing, seamless, elevate, revolutionize, best in class, and "leverage" used as a verb.
- Corporate filler, vague praise, and a tidy positive wrap-up line.
- "I hope this helps", "just checking in", "touching base", "circling back".
- An em dash anywhere.
