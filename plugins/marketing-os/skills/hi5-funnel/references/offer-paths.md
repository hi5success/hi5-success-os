# Offer Paths and Guarantees

Used by `SKILL.md` (Step 5). Show only the paths that fit the member's offer, recommend one, explain the tradeoff in a line, and state the honesty rule for the chosen path. Compliance rules in `compliance-by-industry.md` override anything here.

## Paths

### A. One complete offer
One product or service at one price that delivers the whole promise.
- **Fits:** trust-first businesses, audiences tired of add-ons, and most service businesses where the offer is a consultation or estimate.
- **Flow:** ad or content, then the offer page, then the booking or checkout, then onboarding.
- **Angle:** "everything you need, nothing to buy later." Say it plainly.
- **Honesty rule:** whatever the page promises has to be something this single purchase can fully deliver.

### B. Complete offer plus one optional add-on
The core offer is whole. At checkout there is one optional extra that saves time or effort.
- **Fits:** lower-priced products with an obvious accelerator, such as a done-for-you setup or a ready-made swipe file.
- **Add-on copy:** one headline naming the shortcut, two or three lines on the time saved, and a price of roughly a fifth to a half of the core.
- **Honesty rule:** the core works without it.

### C. Three levels of help
The same result at two or three levels: do it yourself, do it with help, or have it done for you. All levels sit side by side on a single page, so the buyer makes one choice.
- **Fits:** offers that can also be set up for the buyer or taught to them, and services that have a product edition.
- **Angle:** "same result, you choose how much you do." Highlight the middle level.
- **Honesty rule:** each level delivers the same core result. Levels differ in speed and effort, not in withheld content.

### D. Entry offer, then a follow-on
A low-priced entry offer, then one relevant follow-on offer after purchase, and a lighter version if they decline.
- **Fits:** paid traffic that needs to recover ad cost quickly.
- **Honesty rules:** the entry offer delivers its own promise. The follow-on is a different result or a speed-up, never something that belonged in the first offer. Show the path openly. Use at most one follow-on and one lighter version, because long chains erode trust.

### E. Free sample, then the paid offer
A free resource that settles one small problem and exposes the next one, which the paid offer addresses.
- **Fits:** small budgets, organic reach, cold audiences, and list building. This is the default for most service businesses.
- **Rules:** a real quick win in about fifteen minutes or less, an honest statement of the next problem, and a five-to-seven email follow-up.
- **The free resource comes from the member's own offer**, never from a stock list.

### F. Ongoing membership
Monthly access to support, a community, tools, or a library.
- **Fits:** problems that keep coming back.
- **Angle:** sell what gets done each month, with a clear first week, first month, and third month.
- **Honesty rule:** state how to cancel on the page.

### G. Partner offer
A bonus the buyer receives for signing up to a partner's product through the member's link.
- **Fits:** software or services the member already uses and teaches.
- **Rules:** disclose the commission or affiliate relationship clearly and close to the claim. Check the partner's rules on bonuses and paid ads. Never promise more than the partner's product does.

## Choosing
- Cold audience and a small budget: start with E, then move to A or B.
- Ads that need to recover their cost fast: B or D.
- A product that also has a service edition: C.
- A problem that keeps returning: F, usually fed by E or A.
- A tool the member already teaches: G alongside another path.

## Guarantees
| Type | Fits | Plain wording pattern | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| No refunds | Instant-access digital products, low price | "Because you get everything the moment you buy, all sales are final. Here is exactly what is inside." | Offset with a full contents list and a good question section. State it clearly. |
| Conditional | Cold traffic, mid-priced, step-based results | "Do these steps within this time. If you do not reach this specific result, we refund you or keep helping." | Define the steps and how to claim. |
| Help guarantee | Templates, setups, installs | "If it stalls, we will work with you until it runs." | No refund, but it removes the fear of getting stuck. |
| Trial window | New businesses, high-trust positioning | "Use it for this many days. If it does not suit you, write to us and we will refund you in full." | Lifts sign-ups, some misuse. |
| Recommend one | Not sure | Pick by price, traffic temperature, and proof available. | Say the tradeoff in a line. |

Put the terms near the final button and in the questions section. Clear terms build trust.

### Compliance overrides
- **Real estate:** no guarantee about a sale, a price, a timeline, or a result. Offer service commitments only, such as a reply time, an update schedule, or a clear process.
- **Health and wellness:** no outcome guarantees of any kind.
- **Coaching and business opportunities:** no guarantee of income or results.
- **Everyone:** a guarantee must be one the member can and will honor, written in their words, with the terms stated.
