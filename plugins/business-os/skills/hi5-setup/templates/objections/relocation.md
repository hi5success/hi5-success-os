# Starter Objections: Relocation

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

**Note for this client type:** Describe areas by features, commutes, and amenities. Do not describe residents, schools, or safety as fact. Follow the Compliance Guardrails.

## 1. "We'll buy once we're there and can see places in person."
1. **Behind it:** They are uneasy about buying without seeing places, and they fear a rushed decision.
2. **Ask first:** "What would you need to see or do before you felt comfortable?"
3. **Response:** That makes sense, and you don't have to buy sight unseen. We can start with video tours and a short list, then plan one focused trip to see the best candidates in person. [PROOF POINT] If you want more time, we can also talk about renting first. Would you like me to put together a plan for a trip?
4. **Text version:** Makes sense. Want me to plan one focused trip with a short list and video tours beforehand?
5. **Don't say:** Pressure to buy without seeing it. Guarantees about how fast homes sell. Anything about who lives where.

## 2. "We don't know the area well enough to choose."
1. **Behind it:** They are unfamiliar with the area and do not know where to start.
2. **Ask first:** "What matters most to you in daily life: commute, space, amenities, or something else?"
3. **Response:** That's the most common thing I hear from people moving here. I can help you narrow it by features and practical details like commute times, the style and size of homes, and the amenities nearby. [PROOF POINT] I'll give you the facts about each area, and you decide what fits your life. Would you like to start with what matters most to you?
4. **Text version:** Very common. Tell me what matters most (commute, space, amenities) and I will narrow it down by the facts.
5. **Don't say:** Telling them where they "belong." Describing the people who live in an area. School ratings or safety claims.

## 3. "Can we sell our current home first?"
1. **Behind it:** They fear carrying two homes or being without one.
2. **Ask first:** "What does your timeline look like on both ends?"
3. **Response:** That's a smart question, and it's one of the biggest parts of a move. There are different ways to line things up, and the right one depends on your finances and timing. [PROOF POINT] I'd suggest talking with your lender and an attorney about your options, and I'll help coordinate the timing on the real estate side. Would you like to map out a timeline?
4. **Text version:** Smart question. Want to map out the timing on both ends? Your lender and attorney can advise on options.
5. **Don't say:** Promising that both transactions will line up. Financial or legal advice. Guessing at how fast their home will sell.

## 4. "Our timeline is too tight."
1. **Behind it:** The move is coming fast and the stress is high.
2. **Ask first:** "What is your hard deadline, and what is flexible?"
3. **Response:** I hear you, and a tight timeline is stressful. We can break it into a simple week-by-week plan, do as much as possible remotely, and focus on the decisions that matter most. [PROOF POINT] I'll tell you plainly what's realistic and what isn't. Can we sketch the plan today?
4. **Text version:** I hear you. Want me to sketch a week-by-week plan today so you know what to do first?
5. **Don't say:** Promising that everything will work out in time. Pressure. Skipping steps that protect them.

## 5. "We're worried about costs and taxes in the new area."
1. **Behind it:** They fear surprises in their budget.
2. **Ask first:** "Which costs worry you most?"
3. **Response:** That's smart to look at before you decide. I can help you gather verifiable information like property tax rates and typical insurance costs from official sources and quotes, so your budget is realistic. [PROOF POINT] For how it affects your personal taxes, your CPA is the right person to ask. Would you like me to start pulling the numbers?
4. **Text version:** Smart. Want me to pull property tax and typical insurance costs from official sources? Your CPA can speak to your personal taxes.
5. **Don't say:** Tax advice. Quoting costs you have not verified. "It's cheaper there."
