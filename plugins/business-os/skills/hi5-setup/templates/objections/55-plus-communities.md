# Starter Objections: 55+ communities (active adult communities)

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

**Note for this client type:** Talk about the community, its amenities, and its rules. State a community's age requirement as the community's own rule. Never make assumptions about a buyer's or seller's age, health, or family, and never steer anyone by age or family status outside these communities. Follow Fair Housing.

## 1. "We're not sure a 55+ community is the right fit."
1. **Behind it:** They are unsure how the lifestyle will feel, and it is a big step.
2. **Ask first:** "What would you want to be true about a community for it to feel right?"
3. **Response:** That's a good thing to be sure about, and there's no rush. What I can do is show you how a community actually works: the amenities, the monthly fees, the activities, and the rules, so you can decide on facts. [PROOF POINT] Many communities offer tours or events, so you can see it in person with no pressure. Would you like me to set one up?
4. **Text version:** Totally fair. Want to take a no-pressure tour so you can see how it works in person?
5. **Don't say:** Assumptions about their age, health, or family. Describing who "belongs" in the community. Pushing them toward or away from a community.

## 2. "The HOA fees are high."
1. **Behind it:** They are unsure what the monthly fee covers and whether it is worth it.
2. **Ask first:** "Which part of the fee is hardest to justify to yourself?"
3. **Response:** That's a smart thing to look at closely. Let's get the community's documents and go through what the fee covers, like amenities, maintenance, and landscaping, and compare it with what those would cost you to handle on your own. [PROOF POINT] We can also look at the community's budget and reserves in the documents so you know what to ask. Want me to request those documents?
4. **Text version:** Good question. Want me to get the community documents so we can see exactly what the fee covers?
5. **Don't say:** Promising that fees will stay the same. Calling the fees "a bargain." Anything about the finances that you have not read in the documents.

## 3. "We don't want to give up our space."
1. **Behind it:** They are attached to their current space and worry about storage and room for hobbies and guests.
2. **Ask first:** "Which spaces in your home do you use most?"
3. **Response:** That's completely understandable, and space matters. Floor plans in these communities vary a lot, so let's look at the actual plans and model homes and measure them against how you use your space. [PROOF POINT] We can focus on the plans with the storage and flexible rooms you care about. Would you like to see a few?
4. **Text version:** Makes sense. Want to see a few floor plans side by side with the spaces you use most?
5. **Don't say:** "You won't need all that space." Assumptions about what they need. Pressure to downsize.

## 4. "We're worried the community's rules won't work for us."
1. **Behind it:** They are unsure about restrictions on guests, pets, rentals, vehicles, or who may live in the home.
2. **Ask first:** "Which rules matter most to you?"
3. **Response:** That's a smart thing to check before you commit. Every community sets its own rules, including its age requirements, and they're stated in its governing documents. I'll get you those documents and walk through the rules you care about, so there are no surprises. [PROOF POINT] The community's management can confirm how the rules apply to your situation. Would you like me to start with the ones that matter most to you?
4. **Text version:** Smart to check. I will get the community documents and go through the rules you care about, and management can confirm how they apply to you.
5. **Don't say:** Telling anyone whether they qualify. "You'll be fine." Opinions about household eligibility. Any guess about how a rule will be applied.

## 5. "We'd rather stay where we are."
1. **Behind it:** They are comfortable at home and the effort and cost of moving feel big.
2. **Ask first:** "What do you love most about your current home?"
3. **Response:** That makes sense, and staying put is a perfectly good choice. If it would help, I can give you a clear picture of your options so you can decide when and if the time is right, with no obligation. [PROOF POINT] I'll only follow up if you'd like me to. Would that be useful?
4. **Text version:** Staying put is a great option. If it helps, I can send a quick picture of your options for whenever you are ready. No obligation.
5. **Don't say:** Pressure or false urgency. Suggesting they should move. Assumptions about their reasons.
