# Starter Objections: Investors

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

**Note for this client type:** For tax, financing, and legal questions, send the investor to their CPA, lender, or attorney. Never promise returns.

## 1. "The numbers don't work at this price."
1. **Behind it:** The cash flow or return they need is not there at the asking price.
2. **Ask first:** "Which number is the sticking point: cash flow, return, or the purchase price?"
3. **Response:** That's a smart way to look at it, and I'd rather you pass on a deal that doesn't fit. Let's walk through the assumptions together: rent, expenses, vacancy, and financing, using your numbers. [PROOF POINT] If it still doesn't work, we can look at a different price, terms, or a different property that fits your criteria. Want to run it together?
4. **Text version:** Good instinct. Want to run the numbers together with your assumptions? If it still does not work, we will look for one that does.
5. **Don't say:** Any guarantee of returns, appreciation, or rent. Telling them the numbers "will work out." Tax or financing advice.

## 2. "I can find deals myself."
1. **Behind it:** They are confident in their ability, and they want to keep costs low.
2. **Ask first:** "How are you finding deals today, and what takes the most time?"
3. **Response:** I respect that, and many good investors do. Where I can add value is a second set of eyes on the numbers, current comparable sales and rents, negotiation, and connecting you with inspectors, lenders, and contractors. [PROOF POINT] I'm happy to work with you only on the deals where that's useful. Would that be worth a conversation?
4. **Text version:** Respect that. If you want a second set of eyes on a deal, I am happy to help on a deal-by-deal basis.
5. **Don't say:** "You can't find good deals without an agent." Promising off-market access. Anything that talks down their experience.

## 3. "I'll wait for the market to dip."
1. **Behind it:** They are worried about overpaying and timing the market.
2. **Ask first:** "What would a good deal look like to you, in numbers?"
3. **Response:** That's a reasonable caution, and I won't try to predict the market. What we can do is define the numbers you need to see, such as price, cash flow, and condition, so you can act when a property fits. [PROOF POINT] Let's write down your criteria and set up a search so you hear about matches quickly. Want to start with your criteria?
4. **Text version:** Fair. Want to write down the exact numbers that would make a deal a yes for you? Then I can watch for matches.
5. **Don't say:** Predictions about the market. "Now is the best time." Pushing a deal that does not meet their criteria.

## 4. "Why pay a commission when I can go direct to the seller?"
1. **Behind it:** They want to save the fee and may not see the value.
2. **Ask first:** "What has been your experience buying direct?"
3. **Response:** That's a fair question, and I'll be clear about how I'm paid before we start. Buying direct can work, and what I can add is pricing data, negotiation, due diligence coordination, and keeping the transaction on track. [PROOF POINT] If a particular deal is simple, tell me, and we can decide together how much help you want. Would you like me to outline what I'd handle?
4. **Text version:** Fair question. I will tell you exactly how I am paid and what I would handle, then you can decide.
5. **Don't say:** Hiding or blurring how you are paid. Disparaging direct deals. Pressure.

## 5. "I'm worried about tenants and repairs."
1. **Behind it:** They fear surprises and hidden costs.
2. **Ask first:** "Which worries you most: the condition of the property, the tenants, or the cost of repairs?"
3. **Response:** That's a smart concern, and it's one of the biggest risks in any rental. Careful due diligence helps: a thorough inspection, a review of current leases and rents, a repair reserve in your numbers, and a plan for who manages the property. [PROOF POINT] I can connect you with inspectors and property managers and help you build those costs into the analysis. Want to do that for this property?
4. **Text version:** That is a smart concern. Want me to build inspection, repairs, and management costs into the numbers so you can see the real picture?
5. **Don't say:** Promising that nothing will go wrong. Brushing off repair costs. Advice on landlord-tenant law.
