# Starter Objections: Land

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

**Note for this client type:** Zoning, permits, utilities, and soil are verified by the official sources and licensed professionals, never guessed. Never promise what can be built.

## 1. "We'll wait until we know what we want to build."
1. **Behind it:** They are not sure of their plans, so buying feels premature.
2. **Ask first:** "What are you thinking about building, even roughly?"
3. **Response:** That makes sense, and it's smart not to rush. The thing to know is that the land's features, like access, utilities, zoning, and shape, can limit or shape what you build, so it helps to check them before you buy. [PROOF POINT] We can review a property's details and the questions to ask the county or city, so you decide with the facts. Would you like me to start with a checklist?
4. **Text version:** Makes sense. Want a checklist of what to check (access, utilities, zoning) before you decide on a property?
5. **Don't say:** Promising what can be built. Guessing at zoning or permits. Pressure.

## 2. "The price per acre seems high."
1. **Behind it:** They are comparing land prices and fear overpaying.
2. **Ask first:** "What are you comparing it to?"
3. **Response:** That's a fair thing to look at closely, and land isn't always an apples-to-apples comparison. Access, utilities, zoning, topography, and location can all change the value. [PROOF POINT] Let's compare nearby sales and adjust for those differences, so you can see where this property fits. Would that be helpful?
4. **Text version:** Fair. Land varies a lot. Want me to compare nearby sales and adjust for access, utilities, and zoning?
5. **Don't say:** Guarantees about value or appreciation. "It's a steal." Quoting numbers you have not verified.

## 3. "Can we build what we want on it?"
1. **Behind it:** They fear zoning, permit, or utility problems.
2. **Ask first:** "What exactly do you hope to build?"
3. **Response:** That's the most important question to answer before you buy, and I won't guess at it. We should check with the county or city planning office, the utility providers, and your builder, so you have answers in writing. [PROOF POINT] We can build that into the contract, such as a due diligence period, so you can confirm before you're committed. Would you like me to put together the list of questions?
4. **Text version:** The most important question. I will not guess. Want me to put together the list to check with the county and your builder before you commit?
5. **Don't say:** "You'll be able to build." Guessing at zoning, permits, or utilities. Legal advice.

## 4. "We'll look for land on our own or buy from the owner."
1. **Behind it:** They want to save money or have found a property themselves.
2. **Ask first:** "What have you found so far?"
3. **Response:** That's fine, and many people do. What I can help with is the research that matters most: access, utilities, zoning, boundaries, and comparable sales, along with the contract terms that protect you. [PROOF POINT] If you've already found a property, I'm happy to review it with you. Would you like that?
4. **Text version:** Sounds good. If you have found a property, want me to review the access, utilities, and zoning questions with you?
5. **Don't say:** Talking down their search. Hiding how you are paid. Promising anything about the property.

## 5. "Financing land seems hard."
1. **Behind it:** They have heard that land loans are harder than home loans.
2. **Ask first:** "What have you heard about financing land?"
3. **Response:** That's a fair concern, because lenders often treat land differently from a home. The terms depend on the property and on you, so the best step is to talk with a lender who knows land loans. [PROOF POINT] I can introduce you to lenders and help you gather what they'll ask for. Would that help?
4. **Text version:** Fair concern. Want me to introduce you to a lender who knows land loans?
5. **Don't say:** Promising approval or terms. Telling them what they qualify for. Lending advice.
