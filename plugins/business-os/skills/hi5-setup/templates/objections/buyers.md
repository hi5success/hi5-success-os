# Starter Objections: Buyers

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

## 1. "We want to wait for rates to drop."
1. **Behind it:** They fear locking in a payment they might regret, and they hope for a lower cost.
2. **Ask first:** "If rates came down, what would that change for you, and what's your plan if they don't?"
3. **Response:** That's a smart thing to think about, and I won't pretend to know where rates are headed, because nobody does. What I can do is show you what your monthly payment looks like at today's rates and at a few lower ones, so you can see the real difference. [PROOF POINT] It's also worth asking your lender what your options would be if rates fall after you buy. Want me to set those scenarios side by side with your lender?
4. **Text version:** Totally fair to watch rates. Want a quick side-by-side of your payment now versus if rates drop? Your lender can run the real numbers.
5. **Don't say:** "Rates will never be this low again." "Rates are going up." Any prediction, quote, or guarantee about rates or prices.

## 2. "We'll just use the listing agent."
1. **Behind it:** They want things to be simple, think it saves money, and may not know how representation differs.
2. **Ask first:** "What appeals to you about working with the listing agent?"
3. **Response:** That makes sense, and it does feel simple. The thing I'd want you to know is that a listing agent typically works for the seller, while an agent who represents only you works on your side of the table: your price, your terms, and your questions. [PROOF POINT] Rules about representing both sides vary by state, so I'm glad to explain how it works where we are, and your broker can confirm the details. Would it help if I walked you through the difference?
4. **Text version:** Happy to explain how working with your own agent is different. Want a quick call?
5. **Don't say:** Anything negative about the listing agent. That they will overpay without an agent. Legal conclusions about dual agency in their state.

## 3. "Why should we sign a buyer agreement?"
1. **Behind it:** They fear commitment, are unsure what they would be agreeing to, or worry about fees.
2. **Ask first:** "What part of the agreement are you most unsure about?"
3. **Response:** That's a fair question, and you should understand any document before you sign it. A buyer agreement spells out what I'll do for you, how long we work together, and how I'm paid, so nothing is a surprise later. [PROOF POINT] Let's go through it line by line, and you're welcome to ask your own advisor about anything you want a second opinion on. Can we walk through it together?
4. **Text version:** Totally fine to take your time with it. Want to go through it together so every line makes sense?
5. **Don't say:** "It's just a formality." "Everyone signs it." Rushing them to sign. Skipping over any terms.

## 4. "We found it on Zillow ourselves."
1. **Behind it:** They are proud of their search and want to feel in control. They may not see what an agent adds.
2. **Ask first:** "What made that one stand out to you?"
3. **Response:** That's a great find, and searching online is how most buyers start. What I can add is what the listing doesn't show: how the price compares to recent sales, how long it has been on the market, and what to check before making an offer. [PROOF POINT] I'd be happy to look at it with you and tell you what I see, with no pressure. Want me to pull the details?
4. **Text version:** Nice find! Want me to pull recent sales nearby and tell you what I see? No pressure.
5. **Don't say:** "Online listings are usually wrong." "You can't buy without an agent." Anything that talks down their research.

## 5. "We're not sure we can afford this yet."
1. **Behind it:** They worry about the payment, the down payment, and hidden costs.
2. **Ask first:** "What monthly payment feels comfortable to you?"
3. **Response:** I'm glad you're thinking about that first, because it's the most important question. The right next step is a conversation with a lender so you know your real budget, and I can walk you through the costs beyond the price, like taxes, insurance, and closing costs. [PROOF POINT] There's no cost or commitment to find out where you stand. Would you like an introduction to a lender?
4. **Text version:** Smart to start with the budget. Want me to connect you with a lender so you know your real numbers?
5. **Don't say:** "You'll be fine." Promising approval. Quoting rates or payments. Telling them what they can afford.
