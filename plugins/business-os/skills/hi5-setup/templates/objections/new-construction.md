# Starter Objections: New construction

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

**Note for this client type:** Builder rules and incentives vary. Never promise incentives, timelines, or quality. Contracts should be reviewed by the buyer's attorney.

## 1. "We'll just work with the builder's sales rep."
1. **Behind it:** The onsite rep seems convenient, and they may not know that the rep works for the builder.
2. **Ask first:** "What do you like about working with the sales rep?"
3. **Response:** That's understandable, since they know the community well. The sales rep typically represents the builder, while I'd represent you: your price, your terms, and your questions about the contract. [PROOF POINT] Many builders ask that your agent be present at your first visit, so it helps to contact me before you tour. Would you like me to explain how it works?
4. **Text version:** Makes sense. The builder rep typically works for the builder. I would work for you. Contact me before your first visit, since many builders require your agent to be there.
5. **Don't say:** Anything negative about the builder or sales rep. Promising discounts or free upgrades. Legal conclusions.

## 2. "The builder's prices are fixed, so there's nothing to negotiate."
1. **Behind it:** They assume there is no flexibility.
2. **Ask first:** "What have you been told about pricing and incentives so far?"
3. **Response:** That can be true for the home price itself, and terms vary a lot by builder and community. Things like options, closing costs, and timing can sometimes be handled differently, so it's worth asking the right questions. [PROOF POINT] I'll help you compare what's included and what each choice costs, so you decide with full information. Want me to put that together?
4. **Text version:** Terms vary by builder. Want me to help you compare what is included and what each option costs?
5. **Don't say:** Promising that a builder will negotiate or offer incentives. Pressure about "limited time" deals unless the builder confirms it in writing.

## 3. "We'd rather wait until it's built."
1. **Behind it:** They want to see the finished home and fear delays.
2. **Ask first:** "What would you like to see before you feel comfortable?"
3. **Response:** That's reasonable, and there are good reasons for it. The tradeoff is that finished or nearly finished homes may have fewer choices to customize, while homes that are not yet built let you choose options. [PROOF POINT] Let's look at both kinds, and I'll help you ask the builder about timelines in writing. Would you like to see what's available now?
4. **Text version:** Reasonable. Want to see what is available now and what is still to be built, so you can compare?
5. **Don't say:** Promising completion dates. Pressure to buy before seeing it. Guessing at the builder's schedule.

## 4. "What if construction is delayed or the quality is poor?"
1. **Behind it:** They fear risk and a lack of control.
2. **Ask first:** "What worries you most: the schedule, the quality, or the contract?"
3. **Response:** That's smart to ask before you sign. We can look at what the contract says about delays, what warranties are included, and what inspections you can schedule at key stages. [PROOF POINT] I'd also encourage you to have an attorney review the contract. Want me to go through a checklist of questions to ask the builder?
4. **Text version:** Smart to ask. Want a checklist of questions to ask the builder about delays, warranty, and inspections?
5. **Don't say:** "Builders always finish on time." Guarantees about quality. Legal advice on the contract.

## 5. "The prices of options and lot premiums feel high."
1. **Behind it:** The extras are adding up faster than expected.
2. **Ask first:** "Which upgrades matter most to you, and which could wait?"
3. **Response:** That's very common, and it's easy for options to add up. Let's list every upgrade and rank them by what matters most to you, and compare what each would cost to do now versus later. [PROOF POINT] That way you spend where it counts and skip what doesn't. Would you like to go through the list together?
4. **Text version:** Very common. Want to rank the options by what matters most so you spend where it counts?
5. **Don't say:** "Upgrades always pay off." Promising resale value. Pushing options.
