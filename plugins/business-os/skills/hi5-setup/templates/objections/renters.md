# Starter Objections: Renters and tenants

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

## 1. "Renting is cheaper than buying right now."
1. **Behind it:** They are comparing monthly payments and may not be weighing how long they would stay or the long-term picture.
2. **Ask first:** "How long do you see yourself staying in the area?"
3. **Response:** That's true in some situations, and a monthly comparison is a fair place to start. The full picture also includes how long you'd stay, the upfront costs, maintenance, and how rent could change over time. [PROOF POINT] If you like, I can build a simple comparison with your own numbers, and if renting still wins, I'll gladly help you find the right place to rent. Does that sound useful?
4. **Text version:** Fair point! Want me to run a quick rent-versus-buy comparison with your numbers? If renting wins, I will help you find a great place.
5. **Don't say:** "Rent is throwing money away." Any promise that a home will gain value. Pushing them to buy before they are ready.

## 2. "We can't afford the deposit and the fees."
1. **Behind it:** They worry about the upfront cost of moving in.
2. **Ask first:** "Which costs worry you most: the deposit, the first month, or the fees?"
3. **Response:** That's a real concern, and costs vary a lot from one property to the next. I'll lay out the full move-in total for each place up front, so there are no surprises. [PROOF POINT] We can focus on places whose move-in costs fit your budget, and I'll point out anything you can ask a landlord about. Would you like me to start with your budget?
4. **Text version:** Totally understand. Tell me your budget and I will only show you places where the full move-in cost fits.
5. **Don't say:** "Just save a bit more." Promising that a landlord will lower a deposit or waive a fee. Anything that sounds like legal advice on lease terms.

## 3. "We'll just look on our own."
1. **Behind it:** They want independence, and they may worry about extra fees.
2. **Ask first:** "What have you found helpful or frustrating about searching so far?"
3. **Response:** That's completely fine, and a lot of renters start that way. Where I can save you time is narrowing to places that fit your budget and timeline, and checking details like lease terms, fees, and pet or parking rules before you tour. [PROOF POINT] I'll also explain how I'm paid before we start, so you know exactly what to expect. Want me to send a short list to compare with what you've found?
4. **Text version:** Sounds good! If you like, I can send a short list that fits your budget so you can compare with what you have found.
5. **Don't say:** "You'll never find anything good without me." Hiding how you are paid. Pressure to sign anything.

## 4. "Do we really need an agent to rent?"
1. **Behind it:** They are unsure of the value, or of how the agent gets paid.
2. **Ask first:** "What would make working with an agent worth it to you?"
3. **Response:** It's a fair question, and no, you don't have to. What I can do is search places that match your budget, schedule tours, check the fine print in lease terms, and keep your application organized so you can move fast when you find the right one. [PROOF POINT] Before we start, I'll tell you exactly how I'm paid, so there are no surprises. Would you like me to walk you through what that looks like?
4. **Text version:** You do not have to, but I can save you a lot of legwork. Want me to explain how it works and how I am paid?
5. **Don't say:** "Everyone uses an agent." Hiding fees. Vague answers about how you are paid.

## 5. "Our credit isn't great."
1. **Behind it:** They feel embarrassed and fear being turned down.
2. **Ask first:** "Is there anything about your application you want to understand better before we start?"
3. **Response:** Thank you for telling me, and you're not alone in this. Every landlord sets their own screening rules, so the best step is to understand what they look at and present your application as clearly as possible. [PROOF POINT] I can help you prepare your documents and explain what's typically asked, and I'll be straightforward about where an application is likely to be a good fit. Would you like to start there?
4. **Text version:** Thanks for telling me, that is really common. Want to start by going over what landlords usually look at so you can put your best application forward?
5. **Don't say:** "Don't worry, you'll be approved." Guessing at a landlord's decision. Treating applicants differently based on anything other than the written criteria.
