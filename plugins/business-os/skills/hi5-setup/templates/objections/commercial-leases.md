# Starter Objections: Commercial leases

Used by Stage 4 of /hi5-setup. These are Hi5 starter drafts. Rewrite each one in the member's voice, replace each [PROOF POINT] with ONE specific proof point the member gave you (or [ADD PROOF] if they have none), and add the pushback they hear most from this client type. Use only facts the member provided.

**Note for this client type:** Leases are legal documents. Always tell the client to have their attorney review a lease. Never guess at what a lease clause means.

## 1. "The rent is too high."
1. **Behind it:** They are comparing the quoted rent with their budget.
2. **Ask first:** "What total monthly cost fits your budget, once everything is included?"
3. **Response:** That's the right question, and the quoted rent is only part of the cost. The full picture includes any extra charges, how rent changes over time, and what the landlord contributes to the space. [PROOF POINT] I'll help you compare spaces on the same basis and negotiate the terms that matter to you. Would you like me to build a side-by-side comparison?
4. **Text version:** Right question. Want a side-by-side comparison of the total cost of each option, not just the quoted rent?
5. **Don't say:** Guaranteeing that the landlord will lower the rent. Explaining lease clauses as legal advice.

## 2. "We'll renew our current lease without looking around."
1. **Behind it:** Moving feels like a hassle, and they do not want to disrupt the business.
2. **Ask first:** "What would make you consider looking at other options?"
3. **Response:** That's understandable, since moving has real costs. Even if you plan to stay, knowing what else is available helps you negotiate your renewal from a position of knowledge. [PROOF POINT] A short market review has no obligation. Would you like me to put one together?
4. **Text version:** Understandable. A short market review costs you nothing and helps you negotiate your renewal. Want one?
5. **Don't say:** Pressure to move. Criticizing the current landlord. Promising a better deal elsewhere.

## 3. "We'll wait until the lease is almost up."
1. **Behind it:** The deadline feels far away.
2. **Ask first:** "When does your lease end, and how long did it take to find your current space?"
3. **Response:** That makes sense, though it helps to know how long it can take to search, negotiate, and prepare a space. Starting early gives you more choices and more leverage. [PROOF POINT] I can lay out a timeline for your situation, so you know when to start. Want me to do that?
4. **Text version:** Makes sense. Want me to lay out a timeline so you know the right time to start?
5. **Don't say:** Fear tactics about running out of time. Guessing at timelines for a build-out or approvals.

## 4. "A long lease is too much commitment."
1. **Behind it:** They are uncertain about the future of their business.
2. **Ask first:** "What are you unsure about in the next few years?"
3. **Response:** That's a smart concern, and there may be ways to build in flexibility. Depending on the property, options such as a shorter term, renewal options, or rights to expand or exit may be worth discussing. [PROOF POINT] Your attorney should review any of those terms. Would you like me to ask the landlord about the options?
4. **Text version:** Smart concern. Want me to ask the landlord about shorter terms and renewal or expansion options? Your attorney should review the lease.
5. **Don't say:** Promising what a landlord will agree to. Legal advice about lease terms.

## 5. "We'll just work with the landlord's broker."
1. **Behind it:** It seems simpler, and they may not realize who the broker works for.
2. **Ask first:** "What appeals to you about that?"
3. **Response:** That's understandable, and it does feel simple. The landlord's broker typically works for the landlord, while a tenant representative works for you on the rent, the terms, and the space. [PROOF POINT] I'll be clear about how I'm paid before we start. Would you like me to explain the difference?
4. **Text version:** Understandable. The landlord's broker typically works for the landlord. I would work for you. Want me to explain how it works?
5. **Don't say:** Disparaging the landlord's broker. Legal conclusions about agency. Hiding how you are paid.
