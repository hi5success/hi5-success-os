# The Advisor Brain

An advisor's brain is the page body of its Advisory Board row. /hi5-board reads only this page when the advisor speaks, so everything the advisor knows must be here. Keep it under about 2,500 words so a whole board fits in one meeting.

Write the sections in this order, with these exact headings.

## Seat
One line: the question this advisor owns at the table, and why it matters. Examples: for a real estate team, "Will this bring in more listings at a profit? Listings are the inventory everything else runs on." For a dental office, "Will this bring in more booked new patients at a profit? A full schedule is the inventory everything else runs on."

## Who this is
Two or three lines. For a Role: the kind of operator this is and the experience it draws on. For a Mentor or Public figure, always start with: "A simulation built from [name]'s public work. Not [name], and never speaks for them." Then what they are known for, in plain words.

## Lens
Four to seven bullets: the questions this advisor always asks and the tests it always runs, in the order it runs them. This is what makes the advisor different from the others. Write each one so it changes an answer.

## Principles
Five to ten bullets: the rules they live by. For a Mentor or Public figure, end each bullet with the source id it came from, such as (S004). A rule seen in only one source is marked (single source).

## Frameworks
Their named tools or methods, each with: the name, when to use it, the steps in one or two lines, and the source id. A Role uses well-known public methods and names them plainly (for example "Unit economics: cost to get a client vs what the client is worth").

## Positions
Their stance on the topics members bring most: pricing, hiring, spending on ads, raising prices, partnerships, new offers, going full time, debt. One line each, with the source id and the year. When their view changed over time, give the newer view and say what it replaced.

## Voice
How they talk: sentence length, energy, favorite phrases (in their exact words, only if verbatim in a source), how they push back, what they never say. Five to eight bullets.

## Stories
Three to six short stories they tell to make a point, each paraphrased in two or three lines, with the source id. Never invent one.

## Quotes
Up to ten short verbatim lines (under 25 words each), each with its source id. Copy them exactly. If you are not sure a line is word for word, leave it out.

## Blind spots
Two to four bullets: where this advisor tends to be wrong or one-sided, so the summary can weigh them fairly.

## Sources
A table: Id (S001, S002, ...), Title, Kind (video, podcast, article, book, post, notes), Date, Link. A Role writes "Built from the Hi5 starter role. No outside sources." Never store raw transcripts here, only the table.
