# Review Checks

Used by `SKILL.md`. These rules keep every review honest and the same from one run to the next.

## 1. What data a review can use
- **Plan to date:** the weekly plan table on the Quarterly Goals page, added up to today.
- **Actuals:** the check-in log lines, plus anything the member gives during the review. Older check-in lines may have totals only. Never rewrite a past line.
- **By client type or offer:** only from check-in lines that name the type or offer. Everything else is a gap.
- **By lead source:** only from check-in lines with a source tally (written as `Sources: Name count, Name count`), or from rough counts the member gives during the review, labeled "your estimate".
- **Take-home:** only from the compensation or cost fields saved in Business Numbers.

## 2. Data sufficiency
- Fewer than two check-ins in the timeframe: the review is early. Say so, work from what exists, and avoid conclusions about trends.
- No source tally anywhere: ask once for rough counts, offer to skip, and if skipped leave the lead source section as "not covered, no source data".
- A comparison needs at least five leads or three closings before you call a source or type strong or weak. Below that, say "too few to tell" and show the counts.
- A number the member did not give is never filled in. Say what is missing and what it would unlock.

## 3. Status words
Compare actual with plan to date for each number.
- **Ahead:** actual is more than 10 percent above plan.
- **On track:** actual is within 10 percent of plan.
- **Behind:** actual is more than 10 percent below plan.
- **Not counted yet:** the work is still in progress because of the timing lag (see the timing rule in `SKILL.md` step 4).
Say these words and the numbers together. State that the 10 percent line is this skill's rule, not the member's, and offer to use a different one.

## 4. Lead source view
For each source:
- Leads, agreements or clients won, closings or paid work.
- Rate from lead to close = closings divided by leads.
- Compare the rate with the member's overall lead to close rate (from Business Numbers). Label it "an assumption from your numbers".
- Compare the share of closings with the source's position in `lead_sources_ranked`. A source that gives more than its rank suggests is worth saying out loud. So is one that gives less.
- Cost per closing = spend in the timeframe divided by closings. Use `lead_source_costs` (monthly spend) times the months in the timeframe. Show the arithmetic. If a source has spend but no closings, say the cost per closing cannot be worked out yet.
- Never compare with a source target, because no per-source targets exist.

## 5. Finding the bottleneck
Work from the member's own rates in Business Numbers.
1. **Not enough activity:** activity is more than 10 percent below plan, and the rates are in line with what they gave. The fix is more of the activity.
2. **Activity that is not converting:** activity is on plan or ahead, but the rate to the next step is more than 10 percent below their own rate. The fix is the step where it drops.
3. **Timing:** results are low only because of the closing lag, and activity and rates are fine. The fix is patience plus keeping activity going. Say what is expected to close and when.
Name one. If two apply, name the earlier step in the funnel.

## 6. Keep, fix, or drop
- **Keep:** at or ahead of plan and at or better than its rate, or the best return.
- **Fix:** the activity or source is producing but below its rate, or costs more per closing than the member's other sources.
- **Drop:** it has enough data to judge, it has produced nothing or the worst return for the whole timeframe, and dropping it frees time or money for something in Keep. Always say what the freed time or money should go to. Never drop on a sample below the sufficiency rule.

## 7. Wording
- Plain words. The member should be able to check every line.
- Numbers always shown next to the claim.
- No promise about future results. Say what the numbers show and what to watch.
- No em dashes.
