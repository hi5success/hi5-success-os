# Interview

Used by `SKILL.md` Step 3. Scan first and skip anything already known. Ask a few questions at a time, never all at once. Point to what you found ("I see you use GoHighLevel and Gmail, so I won't ask which tools").

## One task
1. What is the task, in one sentence? What starts it, and what does "done" look like?
2. How long does it take each time, and how many times a week or month? (This gives hours per week. Show the math.)
3. Who does it today, and which tools are involved?
4. What goes wrong, gets delayed, or needs to be redone?
5. Does it need your judgment or your relationships, or is it the same every time?
6. What would you do with the time back?

## Full audit
Ask these, one group at a time:
- **Recurring tasks.** List the jobs you or your team do every day, week, and month. For each: who does it, how long, how often, which tools.
- **What slips.** What gets forgotten, delayed, or done twice? What causes errors or rework? What do clients or teammates wait on?
- **Drain and joy.** Which tasks drain you, and which do you love or would never hand off? Use the Self Profile and the member's People page only as a hint to ask a better question (for example, if the profile says energy comes from people time, ask whether calls and meetings are the energizing work). Never decide for the member. Confirm every guess.
- **Judgment and trust.** Which tasks need your judgment, a relationship, or a compliance decision, so they should stay human?
- **The time back.** What would you do with the hours? (This is often the real reason, and it guides what to fix first.)

## Whole team audit
Offer the copy-paste set below for each person to answer for themselves, and paste the answers back. Never fill in for a teammate without saying so. Tag every task with its owner's name as it appears in People.
---
Hi [NAME], [MEMBER] is finding what slows the team down so we can fix it. Please list the jobs you do every week (no more than ten). For each one, write: what it is, how long it takes each time, how often, and which tools you use. Then answer: what slips or gets redone, which jobs drain you, which jobs you enjoy most, and what you would do with an extra hour a day. There are no wrong answers and nothing here is about your performance. Send it back to [MEMBER].
---
Say once to the member: the answers are for finding slow work, and are never used to rate people or to decide about hiring, pay, promotion, or discipline.

## Existing AI and tools
Ask what the member already uses (a Claude Project, a scheduled task, platform automations, other tools) and what it covers, so you never rebuild it.
