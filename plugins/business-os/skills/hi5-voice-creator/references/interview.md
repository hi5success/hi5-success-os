# Samples and Interview

Used by `SKILL.md` Steps 3 and 4. Scan first: skip anything the Master Profile, the Voice Profile page, the personality profile, and the samples already answer, and show what you inferred as statements the member can correct.

## What counts as a sample
Only things the person wrote or said themselves.
- Emails they sent, texts, documents, social posts, blog posts.
- Video, YouTube, or podcast transcripts, and voice memo transcripts.
- Aim for at least 2 kinds of writing plus 1 spoken transcript if they have one. More variety shows how the voice changes by audience.
- Accept pasted text and uploaded files. A rough amount of 5 to 10 pieces across kinds is enough to start. More is better, but never ask for more than the member wants to give.

## What does not count
- Anything written by an AI, a ghostwriter, an assistant, or a team member.
- Copied text from other people, templates, and forwarded messages.
- Marketing copy the person did not write.
If a sample looks AI-written (stock phrases like "I hope this email finds you well", a perfect structure, a list of three with a bow at the end, em dashes the person never uses elsewhere, or it reads very differently from their other samples), say so in one line and ask: "This one reads differently from the rest. Did you write it yourself? Should I use it?" Use it only if they say yes.

## Sent mail (optional)
If an email tool is connected and the member agrees, search sent mail read only for a mix of recipients (a client, a friend, a teammate). Show the list (subject, date, who it went to in general terms) and let the member remove any. Read only the messages they approve. Never send, draft, label, or change anything. Say what you used when you are done.

## Interview: a few questions at a time
Ask only what the samples and the scan did not answer. Four at a time.
1. **Phrases they use a lot.** What phrases do you say all the time? How do you greet people, and how do you sign off?
2. **Words and phrases they never use.** What words make you cringe? What would you never say?
3. **Humor.** Do you joke? What kind (dry, silly, self-teasing, none)? Where do you keep it out?
4. **Emoji and punctuation.** Emoji (which, how many, where), exclamation points, ellipses, dashes, capital letters, lowercase habits.
5. **Formality by audience.** How do you sound to clients, to friends, on social, and with your team?
6. **Openings and closings.** How do you start and end an email, a text, a post, a video?
7. **Storytelling.** Do you tell stories? Short or long? Do you start with the point or build up to it?
8. **Slang and regional words.** Any slang, local phrases, or words from another language you mix in?
9. **Languages.** What languages do you write in, and do you mix them?
10. **Profanity.** Do you swear in your own writing or speech, yes or no, and where?
11. **How you want to come across.** Warm, direct, funny, calm, bold? What should a reader feel?
12. **Anything else a ghostwriter should know** about how you write.

## Show your inferences
After each batch, say what you noticed in short statements and ask whether they are right, for example "You write short texts, no capitals at the start, and sign with just your first name. Right?" Keep what they confirm and drop what they correct.
